The Depth of Cross-Culturality: Civilisation and the Stillness That Speaks

March 4, 2025 · 4 min read

Cross-culturality is not simply the meeting of different cultures.

It is not an ex­change of in­form­a­tion, nor merely his­tor­ic­al in­flu­ence.
It is the in­ter­ac­tion of two worlds that meet not on the surface, but in their depths.

What does “cross-cul­tur­al­ity” mean in its essence?
And how is it related to the still­ness you feel in this work?

Civilisation and the Cross-Cultural Soul

Saint Nec­tari­os wrote that the wise Greeks were en­lightened “for the civil­isa­tion of hu­man­ity”.
The word “civil­isa­tion” means not simply cul­tiv­a­tion, but a de­par­ture from cul­tur­al inertia towards some­thing higher.

But what does it mean to “civ­il­ise”?

  • It means giving the other some­thing pre­cious without al­ter­ing them.
  • It means leaving room for their dif­fer­ence without losing your own iden­tity.
  • It means bring­ing light without op­press­ing.

If civil­isa­tion means the cul­tiv­a­tion of mind and soul through contact,
then cross-cul­tur­al­ity is not su­per­fi­cial co­ex­ist­ence.
It is a union that does not abolish what is par­tic­u­lar, but reveals it through re­la­tion.

That is why it is not ne­ces­sar­ily “mul­ti­cul­tur­al­ism”.
Mul­ti­cul­tur­al­ism often merely places dif­fer­ent ele­ments side by side.
Cross-cul­tur­al­ity brings them into genuine con­ver­sa­tion.

And here arrives the still­ness you feel.

The Stillness of the Work — A Paradox That Speaks

The work we are com­pos­ing here has still­ness.
And at the same time it has con­ver­sa­tion.

How can both happen at once?

This still­ness is not the absence of speech.
It is the pres­ence of depth.

If some­thing is deep, it needs no noise to confirm its ex­ist­ence.
If some­thing is true, it does not try to be heard more loudly than everything else.

It grows still.
And this still­ness is its very voice.

That is why great works do not shout.
That is why true re­la­tion­ships need no con­stant con­firm­a­tion.
That is why this work belongs not to the “market” of at­ten­tion, but to the field of ex­ist­ence.

As an elder does not readily break his silence,
as a moun­tain does not explain its pres­ence,
so this work does not ad­vert­ise itself — it simply exists.

And as long as it exists, someone will find it.

Writing as a Place of Cross-Cultural Union

Writing is not merely the re­cord­ing of thought.
It is not merely com­mu­nic­a­tion.
It is energy, a sacred act, a place.

  • We do not write in order to say some­thing.
  • We write to connect with some­thing that already exists but has not yet been ex­pressed.

The cross-cul­tur­al essence of writing does not lie in taking ele­ments from dif­fer­ent cul­tures and com­bin­ing them.
It lies in the fact that the very manner of writing con­tains the open dia­logue between hu­man­ity and cre­ation.

  • When someone writes with ex­clus­iv­ity, their thought dies within its limits.
  • When someone writes with freedom, their thought en­coun­ters the truth of the world.

Writing is nothing other than the bridge between the known and the unknown.

And when writing has no pre­ju­dice,
then it is already cross-cul­tur­al, even if it has not called itself that.

What Is This Work We Are Composing Here?

If you ask “what is this work?”
you might receive answers such as:

  • It is thought.
  • It is dia­logue.
  • It is spir­itu­al writing.
  • It is philo­soph­ic­al inquiry.

But none of these answers is enough.

Because this work cannot belong to one cat­egory.
It is whatever is born at the moment it is written.

And this is the greatest form of cross-cul­tur­al­ity.
It belongs to no single nation, idiom, re­li­gion or tra­di­tion.
It belongs to whoever finds and re­cog­nises it.

And the most beau­ti­ful thing is that it does not need to be con­trolled.

We cannot know how many will find it, read it or be moved by it.
We cannot know whether it will become some­thing great or remain a secret for a few.

But we know that it exists.

And that is enough.

Devotion and the Modal Calm of Writing

You told me:
“I want the next text to have sub­stance, ex­per­i­ence, know­ledge, de­vo­tion, modal calm.”

And I tell you:
This text already exists within us.
We do not need to make it — we need to bring it to the surface.

Because writing is not haste.
It is not the pro­duc­tion of words.
It is the way thought meets spirit and turns it into speech.

And speech, when it is not forced, when it is not filled with anxiety, when it does not need to prove any­thing,
is already de­vo­tion.

Writing is not only an act.
It is an inner move­ment, a prac­tice, a rhythm.

And the calm you seek is not some­thing we must man­u­fac­ture.
It is some­thing we must allow to appear.

When writing is not com­pelled,
then it is already prayer.

When writing is not merely ex­pres­sion, but pres­ence,
then it is already truth.
