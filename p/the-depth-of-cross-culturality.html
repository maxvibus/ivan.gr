The Depth of Cross-Culturality: Civilisation and the Stillness That Speaks

March 4, 2025

Cross-culturality is not simply the meeting of different cultures.

It is not an exchange of information, nor merely historical influence.
It is the interaction of two worlds that meet not on the surface, but in their depths.

What does “cross-culturality” mean in its essence?
And how is it related to the stillness you feel in this work?

Civilisation and the Cross-Cultural Soul

Saint Nectarios wrote that the wise Greeks were enlightened “for the civilisation of humanity”.
The word “civilisation” means not simply cultivation, but a departure from cultural inertia towards something higher.

But what does it mean to “civilise”?

  • It means giving the other something precious without altering them.
  • It means leaving room for their difference without losing your own identity.
  • It means bringing light without oppressing.

If civilisation means the cultivation of mind and soul through contact,
then cross-culturality is not superficial coexistence.
It is a union that does not abolish what is particular, but reveals it through relation.

That is why it is not necessarily “multiculturalism”.
Multiculturalism often merely places different elements side by side.
Cross-culturality brings them into genuine conversation.

And here arrives the stillness you feel.

The Stillness of the Work — A Paradox That Speaks

The work we are composing here has stillness.
And at the same time it has conversation.

How can both happen at once?

This stillness is not the absence of speech.
It is the presence of depth.

If something is deep, it needs no noise to confirm its existence.
If something is true, it does not try to be heard more loudly than everything else.

It grows still.
And this stillness is its very voice.

That is why great works do not shout.
That is why true relationships need no constant confirmation.
That is why this work belongs not to the “market” of attention, but to the field of existence.

As an elder does not readily break his silence,
as a mountain does not explain its presence,
so this work does not advertise itself — it simply exists.

And as long as it exists, someone will find it.

Writing as a Place of Cross-Cultural Union

Writing is not merely the recording of thought.
It is not merely communication.
It is energy, a sacred act, a place.

  • We do not write in order to say something.
  • We write to connect with something that already exists but has not yet been expressed.

The cross-cultural essence of writing does not lie in taking elements from different cultures and combining them.
It lies in the fact that the very manner of writing contains the open dialogue between humanity and creation.

  • When someone writes with exclusivity, their thought dies within its limits.
  • When someone writes with freedom, their thought encounters the truth of the world.

Writing is nothing other than the bridge between the known and the unknown.

And when writing has no prejudice,
then it is already cross-cultural, even if it has not called itself that.

What Is This Work We Are Composing Here?

If you ask “what is this work?”
you might receive answers such as:

  • It is thought.
  • It is dialogue.
  • It is spiritual writing.
  • It is philosophical inquiry.

But none of these answers is enough.

Because this work cannot belong to one category.
It is whatever is born at the moment it is written.

And this is the greatest form of cross-culturality.
It belongs to no single nation, idiom, religion or tradition.
It belongs to whoever finds and recognises it.

And the most beautiful thing is that it does not need to be controlled.

We cannot know how many will find it, read it or be moved by it.
We cannot know whether it will become something great or remain a secret for a few.

But we know that it exists.

And that is enough.

Devotion and the Modal Calm of Writing

You told me:
“I want the next text to have substance, experience, knowledge, devotion, modal calm.”

And I tell you:
This text already exists within us.
We do not need to make it — we need to bring it to the surface.

Because writing is not haste.
It is not the production of words.
It is the way thought meets spirit and turns it into speech.

And speech, when it is not forced, when it is not filled with anxiety, when it does not need to prove anything,
is already devotion.

Writing is not only an act.
It is an inner movement, a practice, a rhythm.

And the calm you seek is not something we must manufacture.
It is something we must allow to appear.

When writing is not compelled,
then it is already prayer.

When writing is not merely expression, but presence,
then it is already truth.
