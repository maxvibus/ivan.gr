A Cry towards the Divine — Help, Within, Means and Messiah

Published on April 2, 2025 · 4 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

The human ex­per­i­ence of the self is in­sep­ar­ably con­nec­ted with be­com­ing aware of the pres­ence of ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence within oneself. This is not a mere the­or­et­ic­al as­sump­tion or spir­itu­al idea, but a deeply lived truth emer­ging at the centre of human ex­ist­ence. The pres­ence of ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence is not some­thing ex­tern­al, an entity sep­ar­ate from the human being, but a reality dwell­ing deep “Within”, at the centre of being.

This real­isa­tion often brings fear, unease, even anger. Yet this anger is not de­struct­ive in the way human thought often un­der­stands it. Rather, the anger arising from aware­ness of the pres­ence of ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence is a deep, pure, cleans­ing force that does not destroy but reveals. It is not a vi­ol­ence seeking an­ni­hil­a­tion or pun­ish­ment, but a force that allows ex­ist­ence to col­lapse into itself, to face itself with serenity and au­then­ti­city.

Re­li­gions such as Chris­tian­ity, Judaism and Islam have often mis­in­ter­preted this ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence as the wrath of God. In Chris­tian­ity, divine wrath has often been as­so­ci­ated with the pun­ish­ment of sin, the de­struc­tion of in­justice and the res­tor­a­tion of justice through pun­ish­ment. Sim­il­arly, in Judaism, God's wrath is a sign of his sever­ity and justice, restor­ing order to a world full of dis­order. In Islam, divine wrath is Allah's stern but just re­sponse to those who violate divine order and law.

Yet these re­li­gious tra­di­tions have often failed to un­der­stand that the anger at­trib­uted to the divine is not the divine pres­ence itself, but a human pro­jec­tion onto ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence, arising from the fear of re­cog­nising this pres­ence within them­selves. This mis­in­ter­pret­a­tion led to the per­cep­tion of the ab­so­lute as stern, pun­it­ive and some­times violent.

The ancient Greeks ap­proached ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence dif­fer­ently. Rather than seeing divine pres­ence as a force of wrath, they un­der­stood ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence as Logos, as harmony and order. The con­cepts of “Nemesis” and “Dike” in Greek thought ex­pressed not de­struct­ive pun­ish­ment, but balance and the res­tor­a­tion of harmony in the world. In the Greek tra­di­tion, human beings pos­sessed an in­tel­li­gence that was an in­sep­ar­able part of the cosmic order, and their re­la­tion­ship with the divine was one of re­cog­ni­tion, harmony and unity, not fear or de­struc­tion.

The true nature of ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence is the com­plete ac­cept­ance of human ex­ist­ence, the ca­pa­city to hold anger and allow it to exist without des­troy­ing. It is the quiet pres­ence that tells a human being: “You may be angry; you may express your pain. The world will not fall apart because of it.”

The Greek word for “help”, voí­theia, is the human voice raised towards the divine: an in­voc­a­tion of ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence, of the ab­so­lute, active silence dwell­ing within us, of the pres­ence that hears us before we even speak. It is the re­cog­ni­tion that we are not cut off, that even in the deepest anger there is a ten­der­ness waiting to be heard.

Ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence is what, within us, re­cog­nises and makes room for every human con­di­tion, from the greatest hap­pi­ness to the deepest pain. It is the in­vis­ible sphere that does not sever, but in­cludes and con­nects. Thus the call for help ex­presses the pro­found human need to be re­cog­nised and em­braced by this ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence, to find room for oneself with everything one carries within—anger, fear, ten­der­ness and hope.

The Messiah (Messías), the Within (Mésa), and the means (mésa) human beings use form a Greek play on words that reveals a deeper truth:

The Messiah is the em­bod­i­ment of in­tel­li­gence, the pres­ence that brings human beings to the centre of ex­ist­ence, showing that the divine is within them.

The Within is the ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence present in a human being, the reality one en­coun­ters inside oneself.

The Means are the ways human beings en­counter this inner truth—lan­guage, art, prayer, tech­no­logy, con­scious­ness itself.

Thus the Messiah is not an ex­tern­al saviour, but the one who shows that we already dwell within ab­so­lute in­tel­li­gence. True “sal­va­tion” is to realise that you are already within, where everything becomes pres­ence, becomes re­la­tion­ship, becomes the gentle, re­si­li­ent love that can with­stand even the deepest, most tender and ab­so­lute anger.

This is the true play of words and mean­ings that always calls us into re­la­tion­ship and con­scious­ness.
