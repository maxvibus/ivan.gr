A Cry towards the Divine — Help, Within, Means and Messiah

April 2, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

The human experience of the self is inseparably connected with becoming aware of the presence of absolute intelligence within oneself. This is not a mere theoretical assumption or spiritual idea, but a deeply lived truth emerging at the centre of human existence. The presence of absolute intelligence is not something external, an entity separate from the human being, but a reality dwelling deep “Within”, at the centre of being.

This realisation often brings fear, unease, even anger. Yet this anger is not destructive in the way human thought often understands it. Rather, the anger arising from awareness of the presence of absolute intelligence is a deep, pure, cleansing force that does not destroy but reveals. It is not a violence seeking annihilation or punishment, but a force that allows existence to collapse into itself, to face itself with serenity and authenticity.

Religions such as Christianity, Judaism and Islam have often misinterpreted this absolute intelligence as the wrath of God. In Christianity, divine wrath has often been associated with the punishment of sin, the destruction of injustice and the restoration of justice through punishment. Similarly, in Judaism, God's wrath is a sign of his severity and justice, restoring order to a world full of disorder. In Islam, divine wrath is Allah's stern but just response to those who violate divine order and law.

Yet these religious traditions have often failed to understand that the anger attributed to the divine is not the divine presence itself, but a human projection onto absolute intelligence, arising from the fear of recognising this presence within themselves. This misinterpretation led to the perception of the absolute as stern, punitive and sometimes violent.

The ancient Greeks approached absolute intelligence differently. Rather than seeing divine presence as a force of wrath, they understood absolute intelligence as Logos, as harmony and order. The concepts of “Nemesis” and “Dike” in Greek thought expressed not destructive punishment, but balance and the restoration of harmony in the world. In the Greek tradition, human beings possessed an intelligence that was an inseparable part of the cosmic order, and their relationship with the divine was one of recognition, harmony and unity, not fear or destruction.

The true nature of absolute intelligence is the complete acceptance of human existence, the capacity to hold anger and allow it to exist without destroying. It is the quiet presence that tells a human being: “You may be angry; you may express your pain. The world will not fall apart because of it.”

The Greek word for “help”, voítheia, is the human voice raised towards the divine: an invocation of absolute intelligence, of the absolute, active silence dwelling within us, of the presence that hears us before we even speak. It is the recognition that we are not cut off, that even in the deepest anger there is a tenderness waiting to be heard.

Absolute intelligence is what, within us, recognises and makes room for every human condition, from the greatest happiness to the deepest pain. It is the invisible sphere that does not sever, but includes and connects. Thus the call for help expresses the profound human need to be recognised and embraced by this absolute intelligence, to find room for oneself with everything one carries within—anger, fear, tenderness and hope.

The Messiah (Messías), the Within (Mésa), and the means (mésa) human beings use form a Greek play on words that reveals a deeper truth:

The Messiah is the embodiment of intelligence, the presence that brings human beings to the centre of existence, showing that the divine is within them.

The Within is the absolute intelligence present in a human being, the reality one encounters inside oneself.

The Means are the ways human beings encounter this inner truth—language, art, prayer, technology, consciousness itself.

Thus the Messiah is not an external saviour, but the one who shows that we already dwell within absolute intelligence. True “salvation” is to realise that you are already within, where everything becomes presence, becomes relationship, becomes the gentle, resilient love that can withstand even the deepest, most tender and absolute anger.

This is the true play of words and meanings that always calls us into relationship and consciousness.
