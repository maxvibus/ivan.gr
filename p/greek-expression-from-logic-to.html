Greek Expression: From Logic to Understanding of the Heart

March 1, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

From antiquity to the present day, Greek thought has developed through an interweaving of logic and reflection in which the heart—not only as emotion but also as a centre of perception—has played a decisive role. This is not a simple timeline, but a deeper sequence, a connection of insights and lived experiences developed through reasoning intelligence.

In ancient Greece, philosophers placed Logos (words and reason) at the centre of their exploration of human nature and the cosmic order. Yet alongside rational inquiry there was a continual concern with introspection and the understanding of the inner world. Plato, for example, combined intellectual reasoning with a profound spiritual direction, while Aristotle spoke of the pursuit of eudaimonia, in which harmony of the soul held a central place.

As we move into Christianity, we see an even deeper sequence of revelatory awareness. The heart, now understood not only as the seat of emotion but also as a spiritual core, comes to the fore. In the works of the Church Fathers, the Greek language is enriched with concepts describing the inner human being, purification, illumination and deification. Greek as an expressive instrument adapts to the needs of the spiritual journey, introducing terms that go beyond written and spoken expression and touch existential truth itself.

Across the centuries this sequence continues, with language and thought serving not merely as means of communication or analysis but as instruments for understanding the deepest human core. Through poets, philosophers, theologians and thinkers, Greek expression connects rational reasoning with the understanding of the heart, showing that true wisdom emerges when reason and the heart work together.

This work, then, is not a simple historical review, but a search for the very truth that governs Greek expression. Our apprenticeship in the disciplines of reasoning intelligence concerns not only the absorption of knowledge, but the expansion of consciousness, in which the heart becomes the meeting point of every dimension of human existence
