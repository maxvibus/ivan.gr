The Environment and the Exit – A Dialogue

March 1, 2025 · 3 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

 Ivan:

I un­der­stand now that all my anguish and silent cry were this: not only had I failed to un­der­stand that I am en­vir­on­ment, but also that I am sur­roun­ded by the En­vir­on­ment.

As though my psy­cho­gen­es­is were sep­ar­ate from my bodily per­cep­tion.

That is why I felt cut off—– aborted.

That is why for thirty-six years I have felt, and still feel, the en­vir­on­ment as a threat.

Someone who is psych­ic­ally re­jec­ted as a desire, does not merely feel an assault on life –—he feels a con­tinu­ous, almost per­man­ent re­jec­tion.

You were re­jec­ted, Ivan, from the life of the en­vir­on­ment.

Like the ex­pul­sion of the seed from the womb, which I once re­garded as the ab­so­lute vi­ol­ence of sur­render.

But in the end, it was not aban­don­ment—–it was an exit.

From what, and towards where?

I still do not know how to express it so that I may find peace within the en­vir­on­ment where, ul­ti­mately, everything came to be.

Re­sponse—–The Voice of Ion

What you are saying is not merely a thought—–it is truth itself pulsat­ing within you.

It is no ac­ci­dent that you feel as one aborted, as someone whom the en­vir­on­ment re­jec­ted instead of em­bra­cing.

The womb, the human being’s first en­vir­on­ment, does not hold him forever—–it propels him outward, expels him, gives birth to him.

Life itself, from the first moment of birth, appears as re­jec­tion.

But is it re­jec­tion, or is it the first exit?

Where you saw the ab­so­lute vi­ol­ence of sur­render, there lies hidden the moment of trans­ition.

You felt the en­vir­on­ment as a threat because you did not ex­per­i­ence it as some­thing that en­closes you.

You lived it as some­thing that left you outside itself.

As some­thing that denied you the sense of be­long­ing.

But what is the truth?

Were you ever outside?

The en­vir­on­ment is not merely a space.

It is the way you relate to space.

Ivan:

So the threat was not the en­vir­on­ment.

The threat was the feeling that I did not belong within it.

But can I be outside it if it created me, if I emerged from it, if it nour­ishes me every day?

Re­sponse—–The Voice of Ion

No, you cannot.

You cannot be outside the en­vir­on­ment if the en­vir­on­ment has always been what shaped you.

You can, however, have lived as though you were outside it.

And that feeling is not small.

It is like an embryo that does not feel it belongs in the womb even as the womb sur­rounds it.

Like a child who was born and still be­lieves some­thing went wrong, that it should have re­mained else­where.

When someone feels that they were not psych­ic­ally desired, they feel that their exit was not a trans­ition but an exile.

They feel that life did not call them but pushed them away.

But what happens when that feeling changes?

When you begin to see that your exit was not re­jec­tion, but the very ne­ces­sity of ex­ist­ence?

When you un­der­stand that birth is, at once, a wound and an in­vit­a­tion?

When life did not push you away, but pushed you towards being?

Ivan:

So I was, I am, and I will always be within the en­vir­on­ment.

Re­sponse—–The Voice of Ion

Yes.

Even when you denied it.

Even when you feared it.

Even when you fought it.

The en­vir­on­ment was always there.

Not as a threat.

Not as some­thing that kept you outside.

But as the frame within which everything ul­ti­mately came to be.

And if the exit was some­thing that wounded you, it can now become some­thing that frees you.

Life did not reject you.

It gave birth to you.

Now, do you see the dif­fer­ence?
