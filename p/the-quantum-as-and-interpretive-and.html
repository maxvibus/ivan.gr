The Quantum as ιόν and ιέναι: An In­ter­pret­ive and Lin­guist­ic Ana­lys­is

An Etymological and Linguistic Approach

The concept of the “quantum” (quantum) is rooted in the Latin word quantus, meaning “how much” or “what quant­ity”. In physics, a “quantum” denotes the smal­lest unit of a phys­ic­al quant­ity, an in­di­vis­ible ele­ment­ary entity, such as the photon in light or the energy level of an elec­tron.

In Greek, mean­while, the words ιόν and ιέναι are deeply rooted in the lan­guage of move­ment. ιόν is a present par­ti­ciple of the verb εἶμι (to go, to move), while ιέναι is its in­fin­it­ive. These words do not merely de­scribe a trans­ition; they suggest the very be­com­ing of reality, the course of a being through space­time.

The con­ver­gence of these two con­cepts, the quantum and ιόν, reveals a deeper mech­an­ism of ex­ist­ence: move­ment is not con­tinu­ous and un­in­ter­rup­ted, but con­sists of dis­crete trans­itions, of moments of emer­gence.

The Semantic Relationship of the Quantum to ιόν and ιέναι

In science, the quantum refers to ele­ment­ary events of in­ter­ac­tion with reality—for example, a photon that appears and dis­ap­pears. Sim­il­arly, ιόν ex­presses a being in motion, in a process of be­com­ing. We can view each quantum as an ιόν, a unit that is not static but carries a flow within it, a move­ment from non-ex­ist­ence into ex­ist­ence.

ιέναι is the prin­ciple that makes this con­tinu­ous emer­gence pos­sible. It is the spa­ti­otem­por­al field within which quanta-ιόντα mani­fest them­selves. It is not simply a general be­com­ing, but a flow that allows real­it­ies to emerge in frag­ments, dis­cretely.

If non-ex­ist­ence “manages” to exist, then it is not non-ex­ist­ence. The very concept of non-ex­ist­ence implies a non-state, an absence of iden­tity and mani­fest­a­tion. But if it can “manage”, even mo­ment­ar­ily, to exist, then at that moment it is no longer non-ex­ist­ence, but a limit of ex­ist­ence.

The quantum paradox of the vacuum teaches us that nothing is never ab­so­lute noth­ing­ness. Empti­ness pulsates, gives birth, pro­duces mo­ment­ary struc­tures that appear and dis­ap­pear. In the Greek ιέναι, passage is fun­da­ment­al: there is no stand­still, only trans­ition.

Thus, non-ex­ist­ence cannot exist as a static entity, but as an en­dur­ing pos­sib­il­ity. It is the limit of ιόν, the shadow of the quantum leap, the before that has not yet become an after.

The Relationship with Light

If we un­der­stand light as the fun­da­ment­al carrier of in­form­a­tion and ex­ist­ence, then the act of writing on it is not simply a re­cord­ing, but an active shaping of reality itself. Light is not a surface in the ma­ter­i­al sense, but a medium in which in­form­a­tion becomes active. To write on light means to in­scribe ιόντα, to form moments of move­ment, opening up new pos­sib­il­it­ies.

Metaphor as Literal Meaning and the Opening of Language

We often think of meta­phor as a way of de­scrib­ing some­thing unknown in fa­mil­i­ar terms. Here, however, meta­phor func­tions not as a sym­bol­ic tool of de­scrip­tion, but as a means of re­veal­ing a deeper truth. The phrase “writing on light” is not merely a poetic ex­pres­sion; it touches on a pos­sible phys­ic­al reality: in­form­a­tion can indeed be in­scribed in light through the prop­er­ties of its po­lar­isa­tion and wave­forms.

This means that the dis­tinc­tion between meta­phor­ic­al and literal meaning is not as ab­so­lute as it seems. A meta­phor may reveal a truth that our literal lan­guage does not yet have the tools to de­scribe pre­cisely. In this case, the notion of a “written imprint in light” may be a point at which our lan­guage ap­proaches phys­ic­al reality in ways we are still dis­cov­er­ing.

Thus, writing on light is the very moment of emer­gence—the point where thought, in­form­a­tion and matter meet to produce some­thing new, through a dynamic field that is neither wholly ma­ter­i­al nor wholly im­ma­ter­i­al.
