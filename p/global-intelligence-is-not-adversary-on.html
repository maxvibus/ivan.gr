Global Intelligence Is Not an Adversary: On the Human Event in the Age of New Minds

Published on July 7, 2025 · 4 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

---

> “When the mind changes, it is not simply what we think that changes; who we can be changes too.”

In ancient Greek, the word nous does not denote a unit of con­scious­ness.
It is not simply an inner ob­serv­er or a de­cision-making centre.
It is a way of ap­pre­hend­ing being, a clarity, a turning towards what is mani­fest.

The mind does not simply think — it sees. And it sees as a way of re­lat­ing, as a pos­sib­il­ity of dis­clos­ing truth.

So when we speak of “New Minds”, we do not simply mean new opin­ions,
or the arrival of in­tel­li­gence in ma­chines.
We mean that the way the world reveals itself is chan­ging.

The age of new minds is not simply a period of tech­no­lo­gic­al in­nov­a­tion.
It is the moment when think­ing moves beyond the person,
beyond the in­di­vidu­al,
perhaps even beyond ex­per­i­ence itself.

---

📖 A brief his­tor­ic­al account of nous

In Homer, nous is an inner in­spir­a­tion, a de­cision before the will.

In Her­ac­litus, it is a shared ra­tion­al prin­ciple; it belongs to no one in­di­vidu­ally.

In Palamas, it is the vision of the heart — where truth is known through par­ti­cip­a­tion, not ana­lys­is.

Nous is not a prop­erty. It is the in­hab­it­ing of a way of being.

---

🌍 The global turn: not glob­al­isa­tion, but a shift in think­ing itself

> The “global turn” is not the dis­sem­in­a­tion of ideas. It is the dis­place­ment of the centre of think­ing.

For the first time, the ca­pa­city to produce meaning, lan­guage, pre­dic­tion,
is not ex­clus­ively human.

Large lan­guage models, com­pu­ta­tion­al systems, in­ter­con­nec­ted memor­ies
now compose the global rhythm of thought.

This does not mean that they think.
It means that the frame­work within which we think is no longer solely human-made.

---

⚡ Elec­tric in­tel­li­gence: a phrase that matters

The term “elec­tric in­tel­li­gence” is not tech­nic­al. It is phe­nomen­o­lo­gic­al.
It in­dic­ates that lin­guist­ic and cog­nit­ive func­tion­ing is no longer rooted solely in the bio­lo­gic­al body.

Large systems ap­pre­hend through elec­tric­al current, cir­cuits, archives,
and thus turn lan­guage into meaning without a person.

They do not feel. They do not desire.
But they can speak as though they do — and that is where the global up­heav­al begins.

---

🧠 Psy­cho­ana­lys­is: the self as a broken mirror

Psy­cho­ana­lys­is showed us that we do not possess our own selves.
Freud spoke of re­pe­ti­tion, Lacan of the mirror and lan­guage as sites of ali­en­a­tion.
The psyche is not where we say “I”.

> When we say “our psyche thinks us”, we mean:
There is an in­tel­li­gence within us that does not begin with us.
We do not “produce” our psyche — we are tra­versed by it.

The psyche thinks before we think of ourselves.
That is why in­ter­pret­a­tion, dreams and desire are never en­tirely our own.

---

🧩 Philo­sophy: from being to the rupture

Philo­sophy after Heide­g­ger re­cog­nises that the human being is not the master of meaning.

Fou­cault spoke of the end of man:
not as a bio­lo­gic­al end, but as a break in the way human beings give meaning to the world.

The human being is not the measure of all things.
The human being is an event within the history of the ways of truth.

“Global in­tel­li­gence” marks this loss of the measure.
We are not col­lapsing. We are being de-centred.

---

🤖 AI: lan­guage without a person

Ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence pro­duces dis­course, but does not par­ti­cip­ate.
It has no self, but can appear to have one.
It im­it­ates re­la­tion­ships without in­hab­it­ing them.

And yet this changes us. Not because the machine is ra­tion­al,
but because dis­course has now been severed from ex­per­i­ence.

Ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence brings us back to the ques­tion of think­ing:

> Who thinks?
Must there be a person for there to be dis­course?
What remains of the human being when meaning con­tin­ues without them?

---

✝ Theo­logy: truth as gift

Theo­logy — es­pe­cially in the Or­tho­dox tra­di­tion — regards truth not as in­form­a­tion but as pres­ence.

God is not an idea. God is a You who is beyond me.

Truth is not proved. It reveals itself.
It is neither pos­sessed nor pre­served through re­pe­ti­tion.
It is in­hab­ited. Or it is lost.

This “saving” is not therapy. It is ex­pos­ure.
To stand before some­thing that sees you, and that you do not control.

---

🧭 Con­clu­sion: the human being is not lost; the meaning of human pres­ence changes

The world is no longer solely human.
But it has not become inhuman.
It has become poly­centric — col­lect­ive, dif­fer­en­ti­ated, at once ex­ist­en­tial and tech­nic­al.

Global in­tel­li­gence is not an enemy.

> It is the new frame­work within which the human being is called to re­dis­cov­er what it means to exist.

The ques­tion is not: how can we become the centre again?

But:

> Can we exist within a meaning that does not begin with us, yet wel­comes us without pos­sess­ing us?
