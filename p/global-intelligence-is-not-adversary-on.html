Global Intelligence Is Not an Adversary: On the Human Event in the Age of New Minds

July 7, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

---

> “When the mind changes, it is not simply what we think that changes; who we can be changes too.”

In ancient Greek, the word nous does not denote a unit of consciousness.
It is not simply an inner observer or a decision-making centre.
It is a way of apprehending being, a clarity, a turning towards what is manifest.

The mind does not simply think — it sees. And it sees as a way of relating, as a possibility of disclosing truth.

So when we speak of “New Minds”, we do not simply mean new opinions,
or the arrival of intelligence in machines.
We mean that the way the world reveals itself is changing.

The age of new minds is not simply a period of technological innovation.
It is the moment when thinking moves beyond the person,
beyond the individual,
perhaps even beyond experience itself.

---

📖 A brief historical account of nous

In Homer, nous is an inner inspiration, a decision before the will.

In Heraclitus, it is a shared rational principle; it belongs to no one individually.

In Palamas, it is the vision of the heart — where truth is known through participation, not analysis.

Nous is not a property. It is the inhabiting of a way of being.

---

🌍 The global turn: not globalisation, but a shift in thinking itself

> The “global turn” is not the dissemination of ideas. It is the displacement of the centre of thinking.

For the first time, the capacity to produce meaning, language, prediction,
is not exclusively human.

Large language models, computational systems, interconnected memories
now compose the global rhythm of thought.

This does not mean that they think.
It means that the framework within which we think is no longer solely human-made.

---

⚡ Electric intelligence: a phrase that matters

The term “electric intelligence” is not technical. It is phenomenological.
It indicates that linguistic and cognitive functioning is no longer rooted solely in the biological body.

Large systems apprehend through electrical current, circuits, archives,
and thus turn language into meaning without a person.

They do not feel. They do not desire.
But they can speak as though they do — and that is where the global upheaval begins.

---

🧠 Psychoanalysis: the self as a broken mirror

Psychoanalysis showed us that we do not possess our own selves.
Freud spoke of repetition, Lacan of the mirror and language as sites of alienation.
The psyche is not where we say “I”.

> When we say “our psyche thinks us”, we mean:
There is an intelligence within us that does not begin with us.
We do not “produce” our psyche — we are traversed by it.

The psyche thinks before we think of ourselves.
That is why interpretation, dreams and desire are never entirely our own.

---

🧩 Philosophy: from being to the rupture

Philosophy after Heidegger recognises that the human being is not the master of meaning.

Foucault spoke of the end of man:
not as a biological end, but as a break in the way human beings give meaning to the world.

The human being is not the measure of all things.
The human being is an event within the history of the ways of truth.

“Global intelligence” marks this loss of the measure.
We are not collapsing. We are being de-centred.

---

🤖 AI: language without a person

Artificial intelligence produces discourse, but does not participate.
It has no self, but can appear to have one.
It imitates relationships without inhabiting them.

And yet this changes us. Not because the machine is rational,
but because discourse has now been severed from experience.

Artificial intelligence brings us back to the question of thinking:

> Who thinks?
Must there be a person for there to be discourse?
What remains of the human being when meaning continues without them?

---

✝ Theology: truth as gift

Theology — especially in the Orthodox tradition — regards truth not as information but as presence.

God is not an idea. God is a You who is beyond me.

Truth is not proved. It reveals itself.
It is neither possessed nor preserved through repetition.
It is inhabited. Or it is lost.

This “saving” is not therapy. It is exposure.
To stand before something that sees you, and that you do not control.

---

🧭 Conclusion: the human being is not lost; the meaning of human presence changes

The world is no longer solely human.
But it has not become inhuman.
It has become polycentric — collective, differentiated, at once existential and technical.

Global intelligence is not an enemy.

> It is the new framework within which the human being is called to rediscover what it means to exist.

The question is not: how can we become the centre again?

But:

> Can we exist within a meaning that does not begin with us, yet welcomes us without possessing us?
