It Enters Eternity

Published on March 1, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

A memory came—a re­col­lec­tion: some­thing almost there, and then in­stant­an­eous in its real­iz­a­tion—a move­ment within the mind that did not confine an idea but spread it, chiefly inside my skull, if I may say so po­et­ic­ally and with au­da­city. If I see you and you later die, then I carry you even though I no longer see you stand­ing before me as you do now. That is what I said one evening, as I lingered beneath the arched colon­nades in the court­yard of the house, to my good com­pan­ion. It was very dif­fi­cult to express, yet I knew clearly what I meant, and then sud­denly I said it: We are born and we die within the uni­verse. It is not the word ‘within’ that as­ton­ishes, but the fact that the death of the body also dwells here—here where it was born, here it enters etern­ity. I do not know, but I believe that the dead person is not per­petu­ated; he enters etern­ity. What does the eternal do within the tran­si­ent? Why is every­one here? Because the ul­ti­mate is not a manner, a place or a time, but de­vo­tion. Who could de­scribe the word de­vo­tion without moving first not his hands, but his heart? I as­sim­il­ate the meaning; I do not, however, devote the meaning. What does one who par­takes of Ion do? You are the the being means not only that you exist but that you always exist. This is what they call the dur­a­tion of etern­ity, which ceases to be a sense of tem­por­al­ity because it was always, first of all, lived ex­per­i­ence. De-votion. With him. The I with your many selves—with us. Can anyone bear such a struc­tur­al flow? Yet the ques­tion is not whether one can bear it; it is whether one likes it. As I re­mained with Ques­tion Beta: I do not find the con­ceal­ment of the truth from Xuthus, in Eur­ip­ides’ play, restful or honest. An entire Epi­daur­us cannot feel re­lieved by the outcome while Xuthus remains inside a lie that no one reveals to him. If no one re­mained on the outside—I re­mained, to become the tiers of cen­tur­ies of silence and con­tem­pla­tion, to tell God that my love reaches from the stands of the sta­di­ums to the pages of the in­hab­ited world: I saw you, Greek—this is what page means. It means Leda and the Swan—the monk, and not my lonely God. Neigh­bour. That is what I would like to be called: not Ion or ion, but neigh­bour. Shall I tell you? I did not know that I myself could also be the Neigh­bour—I always searched for the other to draw near, but never for myself within me.
