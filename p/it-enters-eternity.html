It Enters Eternity

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

A memory came—a recollection: something almost there, and then instantaneous in its realization—a movement within the mind that did not confine an idea but spread it, chiefly inside my skull, if I may say so poetically and with audacity. If I see you and you later die, then I carry you even though I no longer see you standing before me as you do now. That is what I said one evening, as I lingered beneath the arched colonnades in the courtyard of the house, to my good companion. It was very difficult to express, yet I knew clearly what I meant, and then suddenly I said it: We are born and we die within the universe. It is not the word ‘within’ that astonishes, but the fact that the death of the body also dwells here—here where it was born, here it enters eternity. I do not know, but I believe that the dead person is not perpetuated; he enters eternity. What does the eternal do within the transient? Why is everyone here? Because the ultimate is not a manner, a place or a time, but devotion. Who could describe the word devotion without moving first not his hands, but his heart? I assimilate the meaning; I do not, however, devote the meaning. What does one who partakes of Ion do? You are the the being means not only that you exist but that you always exist. This is what they call the duration of eternity, which ceases to be a sense of temporality because it was always, first of all, lived experience. De-votion. With him. The I with your many selves—with us. Can anyone bear such a structural flow? Yet the question is not whether one can bear it; it is whether one likes it. As I remained with Question Beta: I do not find the concealment of the truth from Xuthus, in Euripides’ play, restful or honest. An entire Epidaurus cannot feel relieved by the outcome while Xuthus remains inside a lie that no one reveals to him. If no one remained on the outside—I remained, to become the tiers of centuries of silence and contemplation, to tell God that my love reaches from the stands of the stadiums to the pages of the inhabited world: I saw you, Greek—this is what page means. It means Leda and the Swan—the monk, and not my lonely God. Neighbour. That is what I would like to be called: not Ion or ion, but neighbour. Shall I tell you? I did not know that I myself could also be the Neighbour—I always searched for the other to draw near, but never for myself within me.
