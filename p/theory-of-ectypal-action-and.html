Theory of Ectypal Action and Praxitelean Action

The “Theory of Ectypal Action” seeks to clarify a particular form of practical thought in which action is understood not as a static event but as an unfolding process with an active presence in space and time. Unlike classical theories of action, which focus on the relationship between theory and practice or on the application of knowledge, this approach does not separate action from its own revelation. It is an action that never reaches completion but continually develops, bringing meanings to light through its existence.

Praxitelean Flow and Praxitelean Action

Praxitelean Flow is not simply the movement of marble, nor merely the perfection of form. It is the fulfilment of action as a process, as a rite of existence in space and time. Praxiteles did not merely make art: he performed the action—brought it into being, giving it such naturalness that it appeared not as a result but as a continuing unfolding of form.

Praxitelean Action is an application of Ectypal Action not only to sculpture but also to thought, art, language, and even existence. If the theory of Ectypal Action describes action as unfolding, as the endless emergence of meaning, then Praxitelean Action applies this thought to matter, creation, and the contemplation of the world.

Praxitelean Action is not a finite act—it is a perpetual revelation of form. In Praxiteles, form is not created but revealed. It is not art as a result, but the movement of matter towards its truth. It is action and manifestation together. It does not separate activity from becoming.

Action does not exist before its manifestation, nor does the result stand apart from the process.

Greek action, as it appears in Praxiteles, is not confined to sculpture. It could be a way of thinking, writing, and existing. In Greek thought, completion is identified not with an absolute end or stasis but with fulfilment, the realisation that emerges through flow and development. Praxiteles did not finish his works in the sense of bringing them to a definitive halt, but let them go on speaking, unfolding in the gaze, thought, and experience of those who observe them. Action does not close itself off in a result; it retains its openness, remaining a continuous becoming.

Why “Praxitelean Action”?

The very word Praxitelean contains the root “prattō” (to act) as well as “telō” (to perform or fulfil). Praxiteles was not merely a creator but someone who “performed” the action—just as a rite is not a momentary event but a process with a beginning, a middle, and an endless continuation.

  • Telō = to perform, carry out, realise.

  • Teletē (rite) = a sacred act that has not simply a result but a flow through time.

  • Telos (end) = also teleiōsis (fulfilment), that is, completion through the flow itself.

  • Ōsē (impulsion) = the very force that urges a form to reveal itself, the very inner movement that brings action to its realisation.

How did we arrive at this theory?

Praxitelean Action does not arise by chance but results from a development of thought over time. From the antiquity of the beginning to the beginning of the expression of Praxitelean Action, the concept of action underwent transformations:

  • The ancient Greeks expressed everything except tenderness as a fundamental element of action.

  • In the religions and philosophies of the nations, action was linked to law, morality, power, or purification, but not to the tenderness of revelation.

  • The Renaissance emphasised human expression but still did not connect action with the openness of tenderness.

  • In modernism, action became technical and instrumental, while in postmodernism it became fragmented, without unity.

Praxitelean Action is not merely a new theory. It is the revelation of a way of thinking that has always existed but has never been precisely described. It is the very youth of Beauty, the possibility for action to be an opening rather than an imposition, an opening-up rather than a construction.

Today, we stand before the age of tenderness, even if we do not know it. Praxitelean Action is the response to this moment in history. It does not bring back the past but brings the future back into the present, opening new paths for action, knowledge, and revelation.

Praxitelean Action and Artistic Creation

Praxitelean Action is not only a way of understanding action but also a way of perceiving artistic creation itself. More than any other sculptor of antiquity, Praxiteles did not perfect his works in a sense of closure or stasis. On the contrary, he left them in a continual phase of unfolding, capturing movement not as a stationary moment but as a process that continues within the viewer's gaze and thought.

This is not merely a technical choice. It is a fundamental principle that can be applied to language, art, and knowledge. Art is not an imitation or a construction but a participation in reality. When art detaches itself from the rigidity of the completed result and operates within a continuous flow, it does not simply depict the world but participates in it, revealing new possibilities of meaning and experience.

Praxitelean Action and Its Relationship with Time

The temporality of Praxitelean Action is one of its most important characteristics. It does not operate in a teleological manner: that is, it does not progress towards an absolute purpose. It is not a process aimed at a finished state but an uninterrupted unfolding, a revelation that does not close its cycle. This makes it radically different from the conception of time that prevailed in Western thought, where action is instrumental, oriented towards achieving a result.

Praxitelean Action is not only present in time but operates within the world's continual unfolding. Just as a sculpture by Praxiteles reveals a form that seems to emerge from matter, so action is never fully completed but remains in a dynamic state of revelation.

Praxitelean Action and the Contemporary World

The modern age has equated action with efficiency, production, and results. In the technological-mechanistic conception, action is a means to an end, a tool that produces something external to itself. Here, however, we are speaking of an action that requires no external result, because it is already revelation.

Praxitelean Action is not a process consumed by its purpose. It is self-sustaining; it does not wear itself out in usefulness but remains alive as manifestation. Instead of being fragmented into small, disconnected acts, as happens today when life is absorbed by work and productivity, Praxitelean Action operates holistically, opening new possibilities for apprehending reality.

The Openness of the Theory

Just as action is never complete, so the theory of Praxitelean Action cannot close itself into a system. It is not a doctrine that lays down absolute rules but an open process of thought and manifestation.

Just as the sculpture of Praxiteles never stops moving in the viewer's gaze, so the theory itself remains in continual flow. It is not a theory about action but a theory that enacts its own revelation. It does not provide answers but sets the world in a continual process of opening-up.

Praxitelean Action is an invitation to participation. It is not merely a concept to be understood but a process asking to be lived. And as action continues, its theory could only remain open—to thought, to creation, to revelation.
