By Teaching Children to Be Silent, We Raise Adults Who Dare Not Speak. Hate Speech Is Not about Hatred, but the Right to Speak.

The Prohibition of Description: When the Truth Cannot Be Spoken

In every­day life, you do not need to be an orator or a politi­cian to en­counter the pro­hib­i­tion of truth. A child may say some­thing they see, only to be told im­me­di­ately that they must not say it.

  • “Mum, that lady is fat.”
  • “Don't say things like that!”
  • “But… she is!”
  • “It's not right to say that.”
  • “So she isn't?”

And some­where around that moment, the child begins to learn that telling the truth is not always allowed.

The Distinction between Truth and “Permitted Truth”

Society does not reject truth itself, but truth when it is stated plainly.

Con­sider the dif­fer­ence:

  • Being fat is not so­cially un­ac­cept­able.
  • Saying that someone is fat is so­cially un­ac­cept­able.

This is not simply a matter of po­lite­ness. It is a broader mech­an­ism of lin­guist­ic control.

➡ De­scrib­ing reality becomes an insult.
➡ Silence becomes a sign of respect.
➡ Truth is pun­ished when it does not follow the codes of social con­ven­tion.

How Does This Relate to “Hate Speech”?

The concept of “hate speech” rests on pre­cisely this pattern: you are not for­bid­den to think some­thing; you are for­bid­den to say it.

  • You are not for­bid­den to observe some­thing.
  • You are for­bid­den to express it unless it follows certain rules.

And in this way, people are trained to fear their own lan­guage.

➡ You are told to respect the other person, not by ques­tion­ing the truth but by avoid­ing stating it.
➡ You are forced to filter not only how you speak, but whether you should speak at all.
➡ And in the end, you in­ter­n­al­ise a fear—not of hatred, but of truth itself.

The Child as a Mirror of the Contradiction

A child says what they see because they have not yet learned to fear their own words. They have not learned that certain truths are so­cially “un­ac­cept­able”, not because they are false but because they are un­com­fort­able.

And when the child is scolded, they do not simply learn not to offend. They learn that honesty can be dan­ger­ous.

➡ This is how self-cen­sor­ship is born.
➡ This is how the pro­hib­i­tion of de­scrip­tion is built.
➡ This is how society learns to pretend it does not see what it sees.

And all of this begins with some­thing as simple as a child saying “fat”.

The Rhetoric of Silence

Con­tem­por­ary hate speech is not about hatred.
It is about con­trolling what may be said.

  • Whether some­thing is true does not matter.
  • What matters is whether you are allowed to say it.
  • And if you do say it, you must accept that you will be la­belled “ag­gress­ive”, “di­vis­ive”, or “prob­lem­at­ic”.

What, Ultimately, Is Hatred?

Might hatred lie not in the wording, but in the way we teach people to be ashamed of their own words?

Might real hatred lie not in the word “fat”, but in being forced to fear speak­ing?

Might society be pro­tect­ing no one from offence, but instead pre­par­ing them to live in con­stant doubt as to whether they can tell the truth?
