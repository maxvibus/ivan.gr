The Laughter of the Suspended Human Being

March 4, 2025

The Laughter of the Suspended Human Being

“Easy, Buddha—don’t beat us up too!”

I do not know who said it first—whether it was the thought, the reaction, or the body itself that uttered the phrase.
But I do know what happened immediately afterwards.

A sudden, wonderful laugh.
Laughter that came not from merriment, but from existence held in suspension.

Laughter is not an answer.
It is the acceptance that there is no need for an answer.

And when it appears,
it is relief and wonder at the same time.
Both clarity and reversal.

From how many perspectives can one behold this laughter?

1. Laughter as a Shattered Absolute

The phrase “I will not sit” can sound like a final declaration.
But laughter allows nothing to be final.

If you cannot laugh at something, you have made it a god.
If you cannot laugh at yourself, then you have allowed yourself to become an idol.

Laughter does not abolish truth.
It abolishes truth’s solemn posturing.

That is why it releases the pressure of the absolute.
Not because it denies it,
but because it does not let it freeze into a single form.

2. Laughter as Theological Vindication

A saint once said:
“If there is no room for laughter in paradise, then it is not paradise.”

Laughter is not a sin.
Laughter is proof that existence is not a prison of meaning, but a living place.

In theology, the demon never laughs.
Only the human being laughs.

Because laughter is proof that life is not mechanical.
It is neither inevitable nor condemned.
It can become an open possibility.

When someone laughs in the face of the weight of existence,
they do not deny it.
They accept it so completely that there is nothing left to weigh them down.

3. Laughter as Anthropological Revolt

The suspended human being does not laugh because they feel safe.
They laugh because they know that nothing is safe.

  • The ancient Greeks placed laughter within the mysteries.
  • The Stoics taught that whoever laughs at the weight of life has already overcome it.
  • Diogenes was wise because he mocked the solemn.

A human being cannot live in permanent seriousness.
They will break.

Freedom is the ability to laugh.
To look at one’s own place in the world and make it a little lighter.

Laughter is a revolt against gravity.

4. Laughter as Ontological Justice

If laughter were unnecessary, nature would not have made it so essential.
Nothing in the universe has survived without some form of laughter.

And when laughter comes at a moment of excessive tension,
then something returns to its balance.

The suspension you felt does not disappear.
But it no longer weighs upon you.

Laughter does not change reality.
It changes the way you carry it.

That is why laughter is just.
It belongs to no one.
It has no master.
It simply happens.

And whoever allows it to happen
understands that the only thing you can do with existence is live it.

5. Laughter as Final Silence

In the end, laughter needs no explanation.
Like beauty, it exists because it cannot do otherwise.

Laughter does not search for reasons to exist.
It simply comes when life reaches a revelation that cannot bear to remain silent.

And at that moment, the suspended human being laughs.

Not because they found answers.
But because they understood that no more are needed.

“Easy, Buddha—don’t beat us up too!”

And then you laugh.
You laugh because laughter is the only logical answer to existence.
You laugh because life cannot be nothing but seriousness.
You laugh because, if you do not, it means you have taken it more seriously than necessary.

And in the end, you laugh simply because you are alive.
