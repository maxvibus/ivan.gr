Release as Flight: When Beauty Seeks No Validation

March 5, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

Beauty fright­ens us—because it cannot be con­trolled.

Beauty does not ask to be ac­cep­ted. It does not press you to believe in it or demand that you feel it. It exists in its own right.

And that is what gives rise to fear.

  • Because you do not possess it—it finds you.
  • Because you do not control it—it changes you.
  • Because it is not an ex­change—it asks for nothing, and that is what makes someone ask “why?”

Release and Flight without a Medium

Release is not simply the act of leaving some­thing behind.
It is not a with­draw­al—it is a sur­render to what already exists.

Flight without a medium is not a fall.
It is not a loss of balance—it is the re­cog­ni­tion that no medium is needed.

Release is what you feel when you do not resist some­thing true.

And why does it sound like “the for­give­ness of sins”?
Because sin is not the mistake—it is the dis­tor­tion of one’s re­la­tion­ship with what is true.

Release is not an ex­tern­al act that “cleanses” you.
It is the ac­cept­ance of beauty without fear, without shame, without res­ist­ance.

Who Has Ever Said “Let It Go” without Striking a Blow?

The words “let it go” have most often been spoken as a command, as a violent removal from some­thing you desire.

Yet true release con­tains no command.
It does not cut you off from some­thing—it lets you enter what has always existed.

And how does it differ from the pleas­ure-seeking “let your­self go”?

  • The “let your­self go” spoken by those who use it as an in­stru­ment seeks to sub­jug­ate you.
  • The “let your­self go” spoken by a fellow human being who stands in truth is not in­ten­ded to pull you any­where.

It is not “sur­render your­self to me.”
It is “sur­render to what already exists within you.”

This “let your­self go and believe” is not a command.
It does not tell you “submit”—it tells you “do not fight beauty itself.”

Accepting Beauty without Distortion

Beauty is not a covert in­sinu­ation.
It is not a furtive in­vit­a­tion con­ceal­ing another in­ten­tion.
It is not a game of val­id­a­tion or proof of worth.

When beauty is pure, it needs no val­id­a­tion—it need neither be proved nor claimed.

And then it is not made vulgar.

  • It does not become an in­stru­ment of pres­sure or con­quest.
  • It is not used to create im­bal­ance.
  • It does not demand that you follow someone or some­thing.

Beauty is already there.

You do not need to conquer it.
You need only bear to see it without being afraid.

And that is what makes the human being tremble.

Not because beauty is ter­ri­fy­ing,
but because you cannot control it—you can only accept it.
