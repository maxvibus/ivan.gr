When Grace Reaches You First

Published on August 11, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

Perhaps you have re­ceived some­thing you neither asked for nor ex­pec­ted.
It may have been a look, an act of care, for­give­ness.
And if you are honest with your­self, perhaps you re­mem­ber the awk­ward­ness you felt.
Why? Because re­ceiv­ing without having “earned” it exposes you—and exposes you at your core.

Theo­logy calls this “some­thing” grace.
And it places it not at the end of a moral effort, but at the be­gin­ning; grace reaches you before you take the step.
It is not your reward; it is the ground on which you already stand.
If you think about it, this is what un­settles you: you do not hold the start­ing point of the re­la­tion­ship.
Someone calls you before you have a chance to call them.

Philo­sophy, on the other hand, tries to grasp the gift without re­li­gious terms.
Jean-Luc Marion spoke of the “given phe­nomen­on”—some­thing that appears without our being able to control it or give some­thing in return.
Jacques Derrida went so far as to say that the true gift is one we cannot re­cog­nise as a gift at the moment it is given, because oth­er­wise we enter a logic of ex­change.

Psy­cho­logy, more down to earth, says that when I receive some­thing, I reveal to myself and to the other person that I am not self-suf­fi­cient.
And this gives rise to vul­ner­ab­il­ity.
Yet vul­ner­ab­il­ity itself is the bridge that brings in­tim­acy.

If you bring all this to­geth­er, you will see that awk­ward­ness before a gift is not a sign of in­grat­it­ude; it is the price of truth.
Because the truth is that your worth is groun­ded not in your actions or your self-suf­fi­ciency, but in the fact that you already belong within a re­la­tion­ship.
On­to­lo­gic­ally, grace is not a “thing” you receive, but the very fact that your ex­ist­ence is a con­tinu­al re­sponse to a call that pre­cedes it.

The dif­fi­cult thing is to stand there without di­min­ish­ing it.
Without trying to balance it out by giving some­thing back im­me­di­ately.
Without cov­er­ing it with words so that your emotion will not show.

Perhaps this is pre­cisely the chal­lenge: to let grace find you un­con­di­tion­ally.
Not because you are passive, but because only in this way can you see your true measure:
the measure of a being that exists not thanks to its actions, but thanks to the re­la­tion­ship that gives birth to it and sus­tains it.

And if someone today looks at you or speaks to you in a way that leaves you de­fence­less, do not rush to regain control.
Stay a little while in that awk­ward­ness.
Because it may be the clearest moment in which you have seen who you are.
