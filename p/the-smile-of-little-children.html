The Smile of Little Children

March 6, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original


A head­line, on the front page of one of the many vulgar daily rags.
“The Wrath of the Lambs.”

Anyone who searches will have no dif­fi­culty seeing
that, even when I speak of Kath­i­mer­ini, I am speak­ing of all the news­pa­pers in the same es­tab­lish­ment milieu, merely under another alias.

Cheapness has more than one name.

Close your shops for four hours, then reopen.

Four hours of protest.
Then, back to nor­mal­ity.

So we do not inform on one another.
So we can show we are still “res­ist­ing.”
So we can count our numbers.

And at the end of the day,
every­one back in the same setting again.

The Vulgarity of Irony and Anger for Sale

And along comes a woman.
She makes a vulgar gesture.
And every­one says: “There, that is anger!”

Because anger has become an image.
Because anger has become a product.

Because if you do not have a power­ful image, you do not exist.
You are not angry enough, in­dig­nant enough, alive enough.

But there is some­thing worse than anger.
And that is needing it.

Justice as Ritual

And what does justice mean?
What does it mean to demand a con­vic­tion?

Will any­thing change?
Will anyone return?

Or will we simply say, “at least someone paid”?

And when someone is con­victed, does in­justice grow quiet?
Is it erased?

Or does it merely change hands, pass to the other side, become silence again?

A Human Being Does Not Need a Judge—They Need Another Human Being

The human is not only error.
The human is the in­con­ceiv­able vin­dic­a­tion that needs no courtroom.

The human is not a polit­ic­al matter.
The human is not a prime min­is­ter, a min­istry, a pres­id­ent.
The human is the human being.

And if demo­cracy forgets this found­a­tion, then what is it?
A well-groomed lie that writes “the wrath of the lambs” and thinks it has said some­thing clever.

The Smile of Little Children: A Position That Cannot Stand

It is not an answer to the wretched­ness of in­sinu­ations.
It is not an answer at all.

It is a po­s­i­tion that cannot stand, because un­der­stand­ing does not always have its feet on the ground.
Because the human cannot fit into the arith­met­ic of justice.

Because a child, if alive, would not say, “vin­dic­ate me.”
They would say, “do not let them make you like them.”
