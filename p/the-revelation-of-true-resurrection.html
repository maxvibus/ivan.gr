The Revelation of True Resurrection: From Spirit to the Repudiation of Violence

Published on March 1, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

From the first moment human beings tried to un­der­stand the world, the idea of re­sur­rec­tion was also born—not ne­ces­sar­ily as a theo­lo­gic­al af­firm­a­tion, but as a deep inner need for renewal. In its most es­sen­tial form, re­sur­rec­tion means a return to pure and true ex­ist­ence. This return con­cerns not merely the body, but above all the spirit and con­scious­ness. It is the moment when lim­it­a­tions, fears and false cer­tain­ties dis­solve, leaving room for truth.

True re­sur­rec­tion, then, is an inner state in which the spirit, free from the vi­ol­ence of pre­ju­dice, emerges pure and strong. In this context, vi­ol­ence is not only phys­ic­al assault, but also spir­itu­al op­pres­sion, ig­nor­ance and the dis­tor­tion of truth. Vi­ol­ence resides in words that wound, in images that fright­en, and in ideas that deprive a person of the freedom to dis­cov­er the light for them­selves.

The re­pu­di­ation of vi­ol­ence is the an­ti­dote to mis­un­der­stand­ing re­sur­rec­tion as pun­ish­ment or as the forced ac­cept­ance of a doc­trine. Rather than resting on fear or co­er­cion, true re­sur­rec­tion emerges as a serene aware­ness of unity and love. It is an ac­cept­ance of the present, a re­con­cili­ation with the past, and a fear­less an­ti­cip­a­tion of the future.

At every moment in history when at­tempts were made to control the meaning of re­sur­rec­tion, human freedom was pushed to the margins. From the polit­ic­al uses of “divine right” to the dis­tor­tion of the spir­itu­al message, mech­an­isms of power tried to impose fear in place of joy. True re­sur­rec­tion, however, is that which re­cog­nises human ex­ist­ence as sacred. It demands no sub­mis­sion. It asks for no sac­ri­fice. Instead, it calls for a peace­ful dia­logue with life itself.

The rev­el­a­tion of true re­sur­rec­tion is the moment when hu­man­ity looks deep within and real­ises that it no longer needs to live under the shadow of vi­ol­ence. It is the moment when lan­guage regains its prim­or­di­al power—to embrace and heal rather than wound. Re­sur­rec­tion is not some­thing we wait to happen only at some future time. It is an every­day event, a con­tinu­al return to truth.
