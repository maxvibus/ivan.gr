The Revelation of True Resurrection: From Spirit to the Repudiation of Violence

March 1, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

From the first moment human beings tried to understand the world, the idea of resurrection was also born—not necessarily as a theological affirmation, but as a deep inner need for renewal. In its most essential form, resurrection means a return to pure and true existence. This return concerns not merely the body, but above all the spirit and consciousness. It is the moment when limitations, fears and false certainties dissolve, leaving room for truth.

True resurrection, then, is an inner state in which the spirit, free from the violence of prejudice, emerges pure and strong. In this context, violence is not only physical assault, but also spiritual oppression, ignorance and the distortion of truth. Violence resides in words that wound, in images that frighten, and in ideas that deprive a person of the freedom to discover the light for themselves.

The repudiation of violence is the antidote to misunderstanding resurrection as punishment or as the forced acceptance of a doctrine. Rather than resting on fear or coercion, true resurrection emerges as a serene awareness of unity and love. It is an acceptance of the present, a reconciliation with the past, and a fearless anticipation of the future.

At every moment in history when attempts were made to control the meaning of resurrection, human freedom was pushed to the margins. From the political uses of “divine right” to the distortion of the spiritual message, mechanisms of power tried to impose fear in place of joy. True resurrection, however, is that which recognises human existence as sacred. It demands no submission. It asks for no sacrifice. Instead, it calls for a peaceful dialogue with life itself.

The revelation of true resurrection is the moment when humanity looks deep within and realises that it no longer needs to live under the shadow of violence. It is the moment when language regains its primordial power—to embrace and heal rather than wound. Resurrection is not something we wait to happen only at some future time. It is an everyday event, a continual return to truth.
