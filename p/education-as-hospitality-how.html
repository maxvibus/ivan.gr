Education as Hospitality: How the Educational Relationship Becomes a Place of Welcome Rather than Assimilation of the Other

Published July 15, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

True edu­ca­tion does not seek to make you the same as every­one else. Its aim is not as­sim­il­a­tion, but the re­cog­ni­tion of a unique­ness that does not need to explain itself in order to endure. Hos­pit­al­ity, in its deepest form, does not say: “come into our space and become like us”, but “come as you are, and let the space take shape to­geth­er with you”.

When the edu­ca­tion­al re­la­tion­ship func­tions as hos­pit­al­ity, it does not impose ad­apt­a­tion, but offers a space in which to remain. The child does not feel called upon to abandon their own lan­guage, their rhythm, their tem­pera­ment, in order to learn. On the con­trary, they feel that even their mis­spellings, their ques­tions, their absence can find room in the place offered to them, not as tol­er­ance but as re­cog­ni­tion.

Edu­ca­tion is not an ideo­lo­gic­al space of in­teg­ra­tion. It is a space of re­la­tion­ship. And for a re­la­tion­ship to exist, it needs a place of freedom—not of con­form­ity. This means that ac­cept­ance pre­cedes trans­form­a­tion. The child does not change in order to be loved, but is loved, and within that love their freedom to grow through edu­ca­tion emerges.

Edu­ca­tion as hos­pit­al­ity, then, accepts dif­fer­ence not as a problem, but as a rev­el­a­tion. The pupil who brings another lan­guage, another body, another rhythm is not re­quired to “fit in”. The place of teach­ing stretches to make room for them. The com­munity is not ab­ol­ished—it is trans­formed by the person’s entry into it.

Where edu­ca­tion offers hos­pit­al­ity rather than as­sim­il­a­tion, the re­la­tion­ship becomes free; it is groun­ded not in control, but in re­cog­ni­tion. Know­ledge does not become a mech­an­ism of in­teg­ra­tion, but a lan­guage of en­counter. And dif­fer­ence is not re­stric­ted—it en­larges the whole.

There, the classroom ceases to be a place of as­sess­ment. It becomes a place of shared dwell­ing. And every child who enters knows that even if they change, they will change not because they were pres­sured, but because they were wel­comed. And this is the essence of every form­at­ive process: not making the other like ourselves, but our en­counter in the common place that comes into being because we were not afraid to remain dif­fer­ent.
