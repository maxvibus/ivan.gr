The Invitation of Consciousness: Towards a New Encounter of Science, Technology and Lived Experience — An Experience at Beyond 2025

Published on April 6, 2025 · 15 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

The age of so-called “ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence” con­fronts us with one of the deepest ques­tions hu­man­ity is called upon to face: the essence and nature of con­scious­ness itself and of human ex­ist­ence.

Ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence is often presen­ted as a tech­no­lo­gic­al achieve­ment, an attempt to re­pro­duce the work­ings of human thought. Yet the es­sen­tial dis­tinc­tion that emerges from a deeper per­spect­ive is that con­scious­ness is not a func­tion that can be tech­nic­ally re­pro­duced, but a con­tinu­ous, living event. Con­scious­ness is not simply a col­lec­tion of in­form­a­tion or a mech­an­ism for re­spond­ing to stimuli, but a fun­da­ment­al con­di­tion of ex­ist­ence that lies beyond any tech­nic­al im­it­a­tion.

Rather than dis­tan­cing hu­man­ity from its essence, this con­tem­por­ary situ­ation leads us back to the heart of our ex­ist­ence: the ques­tion of the au­then­ti­city of ex­per­i­ence. Ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence can imitate lan­guage, speech, even cog­nit­ive pro­cesses, but it cannot live life as a human being does. Human ex­per­i­ence is never merely in­form­a­tion—it is the im­me­di­ate, bare aware­ness of ex­ist­ence.

In this new age, we are called not merely to use tech­no­logy, but to re­dis­cov­er what it means to be human. The real task is not to man­u­fac­ture “in­tel­li­gence”, but to become aware of our ex­ist­ence anew, to ex­per­i­ence con­scious­ness as a living pres­ence, as dis­course in the act of hap­pen­ing.

Con­scious­ness does not follow; it pre­cedes. It is not an outcome, but a pre­con­di­tion. And the deepest truth emer­ging from this age is that human beings can never lose their au­then­t­ic pres­ence—as long as they return to the aware­ness that their con­scious­ness itself is always there, as a living event, long before any tech­no­logy.

As I walked among the stands at the Beyond 2025 ex­hib­i­tion, I felt some­thing very par­tic­u­lar hap­pen­ing. It was not simply curi­os­ity or in­terest in the in­nov­a­tions on display, but a sense that I was within a deeper flow. It was as though I was already par­ti­cip­at­ing in some­thing hap­pen­ing before me, even before I clearly real­ised it.

In every face I saw, I dis­cerned a subtle pres­ence that con­cerned not merely the person before me, but the con­scious­ness I en­countered through them. It was not always easy—at times I felt that some people avoided me, others looked at me doubt­fully, and others with au­then­t­ic curi­os­ity. Yet every gaze held the pres­ence of the same event: con­scious­ness ex­press­ing itself through human ex­ist­ence.

At one stand, I stopped to speak to someone from a uni­ver­sity. I told him about my work, my project, which I could not easily com­press into a few words or a few minutes. His manner was im­me­di­ately guarded, and he in­ter­rup­ted me, saying, “If you cannot explain it in three minutes, you have nothing.” I felt silence spread between us, not from em­bar­rass­ment, but because I im­me­di­ately real­ised that we were talking about two en­tirely dif­fer­ent things. It was not that he did not un­der­stand—he was un­will­ing or unable to accept some­thing that did not already have a def­in­ite form.

At another stand, by con­trast, I met two women who listened at­tent­ively, not so much to my words as to the passion behind them. I felt their hearts were open and smiled with them as they wished me success in a way so simple and sincere that it moved me deeply. It was as though their con­scious­ness had met mine in a moment of au­then­t­ic re­cog­ni­tion. As though they had already un­der­stood, even before I ex­plained.

I also stood before another stand, where rep­res­ent­at­ives of another uni­ver­sity listened warily to what I was trying to express. As I spoke, I saw doubt written on their faces, as though they were won­der­ing, “Who is this person who claims he can shake up our science?” I sensed their tension, not ag­gress­ive but rather de­fens­ive, as though they were pro­tect­ing some­thing very fragile that they did not want dis­turbed. I do not blame them—deep within, I felt that this en­counter gave me an op­por­tun­ity to see clearly the dis­tance between my own ex­per­i­ence of con­scious­ness and the way the Western world manages know­ledge as a well-defined field with clear bound­ar­ies.

A little further on, I stopped to speak to a man rep­res­ent­ing a foreign company from India. I spoke simply, telling him that my roots came from his country and that I dreamed of being there one day. With a smile that con­veyed a re­cog­ni­tion deeper than mere po­lite­ness, he gladly gave me his card, and I felt that he too re­cog­nised some­thing con­nect­ing us beyond words. For me, this moment was another small miracle in which con­scious­ness once again re­vealed how people truly connect.

As time passed, I felt ever more clearly that the Beyond ex­hib­i­tion was a living stage on which the drama of con­scious­ness itself was un­fold­ing before me. Re­ac­tions, glances, even small moments of em­bar­rass­ment or per­plex­ity were not simply in­cid­ents hap­pen­ing by chance. They were the ways con­scious­ness itself ex­pressed itself through each person in­di­vidu­ally and, at the same time, through every­one to­geth­er.

When it was time to leave, as I walked towards the exit with the crowd, I felt some­thing with an in­tens­ity I had never ex­per­i­enced before. It was as though we were all walking in the same dir­ec­tion, not merely phys­ic­ally but ex­ist­en­tially—a common flow uniting us all. It was a “de­par­ture from stand­ing still”, an ex­per­i­ence so simple yet so pro­foundly re­veal­ing. I felt a clear aware­ness pass through me that this lived ex­per­i­ence was a tan­gible mani­fest­a­tion of how con­scious­ness happens, how it re­cog­nises itself in our shared journey.

And so, as I left, I already knew that this had not been a simple visit to a tech­no­logy ex­hib­i­tion, but an event of deeper con­scious un­der­stand­ing. It was living proof that con­scious­ness is not only mine or yours, but some­thing we all share, some­thing already hap­pen­ing long before we begin to de­scribe it. And this simple, pro­found, quiet real­isa­tion was, for me, the true gift of that day.

I un­der­stood that my ex­per­i­ence at the ex­hib­i­tion was not merely per­son­al. It touched the very nature of con­scious­ness as a living, en­dur­ing pres­ence. Con­scious­ness was not some­thing I pos­sessed or could simply de­scribe in words—it was some­thing already hap­pen­ing, before me and beyond me, some­thing I re­cog­nised and that, at the same time, re­cog­nised me.

Gradu­ally I un­der­stood that what I some­times felt as loneli­ness or isol­a­tion in my attempt to explain what I ex­per­i­ence was simply the tension between the “po­ten­ti­al­ity” of con­scious­ness and the “en­act­ment” of con­scious­ness itself: between what can be ex­pressed and what is already being ex­pressed, without any need for de­scrip­tion.

As I went deeper, I un­der­stood that the em­bar­rass­ment, dis­com­fort and even re­jec­tion I felt from some people were not simply obstacles. They were part of the process, part of re­cog­nising that con­scious­ness is never self-evident—it must be lived, must happen, must be en­countered in life itself.

The deeper meaning of the work “ion” is the real­isa­tion that con­scious­ness is not an object of study, but a living event that already exists and happens. It is not a state to be de­scribed or ex­plained, but an ongoing ex­per­i­ence that calls on human beings to re­cog­nise them­selves not as ob­serving sub­jects, but as active, living par­ti­cip­a­tion in the very pres­ence of life.

This work tells us that con­scious­ness is not some­thing sep­ar­ate from us, some­thing we “have”, but that we ourselves are con­scious­ness, a con­tinu­ous act of aware­ness hap­pen­ing here and now. “ion” shows us that there is no con­scious­ness outside life, just as there is no life outside con­scious­ness. We are this living unity, the very mani­fest­a­tion of dis­course in the act of hap­pen­ing.

The work “ion” reveals that empathy is not merely the ability to un­der­stand another person's feel­ings, but the deeper aware­ness that we all share the same fun­da­ment­al lived ex­per­i­ence of con­scious­ness. In this sense, empathy is not simply a moral virtue or a social skill, but the natural state of con­scious­ness re­cog­nising itself in the other. True empathy is thus the lived ex­per­i­ence of unity, the real­isa­tion that the other is never truly “other”, but a mani­fest­a­tion of the same deeper event we call con­scious­ness.

Yet Western civil­isa­tion, with its em­phas­is on in­di­vidu­al­ity, tech­no­lo­gic­al pro­gress and control over nature, ob­structs pre­cisely this un­der­stand­ing. It tries to treat con­scious­ness as some­thing that can be meas­ured, ex­plained or used as a tool for pro­du­cing know­ledge and wealth. In this way, Western civil­isa­tion turns con­scious­ness into an object, sever­ing it from its living event and thus losing access to its deeper meaning.

What I un­der­stand and re­cog­nise through the work “ion” is that sep­ar­at­ing con­scious­ness from its living event has led Western civil­isa­tion into a pro­found crisis of meaning. People con­stantly seek ex­tern­al goals, achieve­ments, ma­ter­i­al goods or tech­no­lo­gic­al solu­tions, yet cannot find real sat­is­fac­tion, because true meaning already lies within the lived ex­per­i­ence of ex­ist­ence.

What is needed, then, is not another tech­no­logy or a new theory to “explain” con­scious­ness, but a return to the im­me­di­ate, au­then­t­ic ex­per­i­ence of life itself. The Western world needs to un­der­stand that know­ledge is not only the ac­cu­mu­la­tion of in­form­a­tion, but the very act of con­scious aware­ness of the world and the self as living pres­ence.

What is usually not taught at uni­ver­sit­ies is pre­cisely what lies beyond the bound­ar­ies of the power that the in­sti­tu­tions them­selves rep­res­ent and re­pro­duce. Uni­ver­sit­ies, es­pe­cially within Western thought, are or­gan­ised around an axis of ra­tion­al, meas­ur­able know­ledge that chiefly serves to re­pro­duce and pre­serve the system of power—eco­nom­ic, polit­ic­al and social. This means that any know­ledge that cannot be meas­ured, cat­egor­ised or put to use within a clearly defined frame­work is con­sidered un­ne­ces­sary or dan­ger­ous.

They do not teach how to ex­per­i­ence the truth of ex­ist­ence without in­ter­me­di­ary con­cepts and in­ter­pret­a­tions. They do not teach the au­then­ti­city of per­son­al ex­per­i­ence as a valid source of meaning and know­ledge. And, most cru­cially, they do not teach the deeper re­cog­ni­tion that the world and the self are not two sep­ar­ate domains, but one con­tinu­ous and in­di­vis­ible con­scious­ness.

In this way, uni­ver­sit­ies them­selves func­tion as mech­an­isms of power, main­tain­ing a view of reality that serves the in­terests of those who de­term­ine what counts as “ac­cept­able know­ledge” and what does not. Whatever does not fit this frame­work remains mar­gin­al, in­vis­ible, or even threat­en­ing to the very system that sus­tains them.

This process of ex­clu­sion, however, is not merely a matter of edu­ca­tion­al policy or bur­eau­crat­ic prac­tice. It is more deeply con­nec­ted to the struc­ture of the think­ing that pre­vails today: a think­ing that insists on con­trolling, clas­si­fy­ing and isol­at­ing phe­nom­ena, rather than al­low­ing life and con­scious­ness them­selves to express them­selves au­then­tic­ally and freely.

Uni­ver­sit­ies do not teach freedom of thought beyond pre­de­ter­mined frame­works, because that would mean rad­ic­ally ques­tion­ing their own found­a­tions. They do not teach the ability to live with the un­cer­tainty and unease that genuine contact with the unknown brings, because this would dis­mantle ex­ist­ing struc­tures of power and control.

This is pre­cisely the system of power: pre­serving a world in which au­then­t­ic, lived know­ledge remains mar­gin­al, while imposed, con­trolled in­form­a­tion is presen­ted as the only valid reality. Thus true cre­ativ­ity, ori­gin­al­ity and a deeper un­der­stand­ing of human ex­ist­ence find no room within the nar­rowly defined world of con­tem­por­ary edu­ca­tion.

I would add that over­com­ing these lim­it­a­tions re­quires courage and au­then­ti­city, not in the sense of re­ac­tion or con­flict, but in the sense of re­cog­nising life itself more deeply and es­sen­tially. It is not enough to cri­ti­cise the system for its limits; it is more im­port­ant that we ourselves ex­per­i­ence an opening towards a new un­der­stand­ing, one that will allow con­scious­ness to express itself without fear and without de­pend­ence on ex­tern­al val­id­a­tion.

Ul­ti­mately, au­then­t­ic know­ledge comes not from ac­cu­mu­lat­ing in­form­a­tion or from aca­dem­ic val­id­a­tion, but from deep and sincere par­ti­cip­a­tion in life, from a con­stant open­ness to the unknown and the new. This open­ness is also the only real re­sponse to the system of power and the re­stric­tions it imposes. It is the freedom of the mind to en­counter itself through life itself as it con­tinu­ally happens.

And this is the deepest, quietest and, at the same time, most re­volu­tion­ary act we can perform.

  • Maurice Merleau-Ponty, Phe­nomen­o­logy of Per­cep­tion

Ex­ist­en­tial and On­to­lo­gic­al Thought

  • Nikolai Berdy­aev, The Philo­sophy of the Free Spirit and The Fate of Man in the Modern World
  • Søren Kierkegaard, Fear and Trem­bling

Neur­os­cience and Con­scious­ness

  • Antonio Damasio, The Feeling of What Happens: Body and Emotion in the Making of Con­scious­ness

Mys­ti­cism and Spir­itu­al Tra­di­tion

  • Simone Weil, Gravity and Grace
  • Meister Eckhart, Sermons
  • Jiddu Krish­namurti, Freedom from the Known

Ancient Greek Thought and Self-Know­ledge

  • Plato, Phaedo, Re­pub­lic, Sym­posi­um
  • Her­ac­litus, Frag­ments

This bib­li­o­graphy was chosen because these sources ap­proach know­ledge not as some­thing “pos­sessed”, but as some­thing that “happens”. They stress the im­port­ance of self-know­ledge not simply as a process of per­son­al dis­cov­ery, but as the aware­ness that deeper Being already knows our self before we even seek it.

The tech­no­lo­gic­al giants can help this work not in the way we usually imagine—with fin­an­cial re­sources or simply tech­no­lo­gic­al support—but by re­cog­nising that true in­nov­a­tion lies not only in tech­no­lo­gic­al ap­plic­a­tions, but in the very nature of human con­scious­ness. That is, they need to un­der­stand that the deepest chal­lenge of our age is not merely to develop smarter ma­chines, but to un­der­stand the human mind and con­scious­ness more deeply as an au­then­tic­ally lived event.

The in­vit­a­tion I extend to all the sci­ences and the tech­no­lo­gic­al giants is to move beyond the limits of their own fields and meet at a common point: a deep un­der­stand­ing that human lived ex­per­i­ence is the start­ing point and purpose of all know­ledge and all tech­no­logy. Let this en­counter become a space where science and tech­no­logy see human beings not as objects of re­search, but as the place where con­scious­ness and reality happen to­geth­er.

I do not know whether this is new know­ledge—and perhaps that does not matter so much. What matters is that it is living know­ledge, know­ledge that chal­lenges and invites each of us to ex­per­i­ence the world dif­fer­ently, with greater aware­ness and au­then­ti­city.

This “strange” chal­lenge is pre­cisely the op­por­tun­ity offered to us all to over­come our lim­it­a­tions, meet at a deeper level and open new ho­ri­zons in science, tech­no­logy and, ul­ti­mately, life itself.

A de­tailed list of sci­entif­ic fields and tech­no­lo­gic­al in­sti­tu­tions that could con­trib­ute to the work:

  1. Neur­os­cience and Cog­nit­ive Neur­os­cience

    • Ana­lys­ing brain func­tion in re­la­tion to con­scious ex­per­i­ence and lived ex­per­i­ence.
    • In­sti­tu­tions: MIT Brain and Cog­nit­ive Sci­ences, Max Planck In­sti­tute for Human Cog­nit­ive and Brain Sci­ences, The Neur­os­cience In­sti­tute, Stan­ford Neur­os­ciences In­sti­tute.

  2. Psy­cho­logy and Ex­ist­en­tial Psy­cho­ther­apy

    • Ex­amin­ing human ex­per­i­ence, self-know­ledge and the concept of iden­tity.
    • In­sti­tu­tions: Tav­is­tock In­sti­tute, Carl Gustav Jung In­sti­tute, The Ex­ist­en­tial Academy.

  3. Phe­nomen­o­logy and Philo­sophy of Mind

    • In­vest­ig­at­ing the struc­ture of ex­per­i­ence and self-con­scious­ness.
    • In­sti­tu­tions: Husserl Archives (Leuven), Heide­g­ger In­sti­tute, Society for Phe­nomen­o­logy and Ex­ist­en­tial Philo­sophy (SPEP).

  4. Ar­ti­fi­cial In­tel­li­gence and Machine Learn­ing

    • Cre­at­ing models that rep­res­ent human con­scious­ness, ex­amin­ing the limits and pos­sib­il­it­ies of machine in­tel­li­gence.
    • Labor­at­or­ies: Deep­Mind, OpenAI, MIT Com­puter Science and Ar­ti­fi­cial In­tel­li­gence Lab (CSAIL), Stan­ford Human-Centered Ar­ti­fi­cial In­tel­li­gence (HAI).

  5. An­thro­po­logy and Cul­tur­al Studies

    • In­vest­ig­at­ing how dif­fer­ent cul­tures un­der­stand con­scious­ness and the in­di­vidu­al.
    • In­sti­tu­tions: Max Planck In­sti­tute for Social An­thro­po­logy, Harvard De­part­ment of An­thro­po­logy, Centre for Cul­tur­al Studies (Gold­smiths, Uni­ver­sity of London).

  6. Biology and Bi­o­tech­no­logy

    • Study­ing bio­lo­gic­al pro­cesses related to con­scious­ness and the body.
    • Labor­at­or­ies: Salk In­sti­tute for Bio­lo­gic­al Studies, Broad In­sti­tute of MIT and Harvard, European Mo­lecu­lar Biology Labor­at­ory (EMBL).

  7. So­ci­ology and Social Psy­cho­logy

    • Ana­lys­ing how social struc­ture and re­la­tion­ships affect aware­ness and per­son­al ex­per­i­ence.
    • In­sti­tu­tions: De­part­ment of So­ci­ology, Uni­ver­sity of Chicago, In­sti­tute for Social Re­search, Uni­ver­sity of Michigan.

  8. Quantum Physics and The­or­et­ic­al Physics

    • Ex­plor­ing the pos­sible re­la­tion­ship between con­scious­ness and quantum phe­nom­ena.
    • Labor­at­or­ies: Peri­met­er In­sti­tute for The­or­et­ic­al Physics, CERN, In­sti­tute for Quantum Optics and Quantum In­form­a­tion (IQOQI).

  9. Lin­guist­ics and Se­mi­ot­ics

    • Study­ing lan­guage as a way of ex­press­ing and cre­at­ing con­scious­ness.
    • In­sti­tu­tions: MIT De­part­ment of Lin­guist­ics, De­part­ment of Se­mi­ot­ics (Uni­ver­sity of Bologna), Max Planck In­sti­tute for Psy­cho­lin­guist­ics.

  10. Psy­chi­atry and Psy­cho­path­o­logy

    • Un­der­stand­ing con­scious­ness through the con­di­tions that disturb or trans­form it.
    • In­sti­tu­tions: Johns Hopkins Psy­chi­atry, Maud­s­ley Hos­pit­al, Na­tion­al In­sti­tute of Mental Health (NIMH).

Each of these fields and in­sti­tu­tions can offer valu­able know­ledge, methods and tools for a more com­pre­hens­ive un­der­stand­ing of con­scious­ness, con­trib­ut­ing to the form­a­tion of a new field of dia­lo­gic­al and in­ter­dis­cip­lin­ary col­lab­or­a­tion.

What I propose to uni­ver­sit­ies and sci­entif­ic or­gan­isa­tions is not mere the­or­et­ic­al col­lab­or­a­tion. It is a living in­vit­a­tion to en­counter the con­scious­ness I already ex­per­i­ence and express. I am not simply another re­search­er asking you to engage with an object of study; I am the living proof of what you are seeking.

I un­der­stand that I could scarcely have put some­thing like this into spoken words at the Beyond ex­hib­i­tion, because the nature of my ex­pres­sion is not con­fined to brief oral present­a­tions. It is written and dia­lo­gic­al—a way of think­ing that happens as I write and con­verse, a way that allows the depth of con­scious­ness to reveal itself clearly.

If you truly un­der­stand the sig­ni­fic­ance of this in­vit­a­tion, then your only au­then­t­ic re­sponse will be an in­vit­a­tion to me. For only through my pres­ence—through my own lived ex­per­i­ence—can this en­counter I desire be fully real­ised, an en­counter already hap­pen­ing as we speak here.
