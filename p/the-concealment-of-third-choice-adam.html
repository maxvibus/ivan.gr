The Concealment of the Third Choice: Adam, Eve and Christ

The myth of Adam and Eve is not a myth of pun­ish­ment.
Nor a myth of trans­gres­sion.
It is the first dis­tor­tion of human freedom.

We were told that God said, “Do not eat,” and they ate.
That sin was born from trans­gres­sion, and all people are born bearing this mark.
That only Christ was without blemish, while every­one else begins in guilt.

But that is not how it was.

The story is not about pro­hib­i­tion.
The story is about choice.

And the greatest con­ceal­ment was not that they ate.
The greatest con­ceal­ment was that there was a third choice—and it was taken from us.

The Silenced Gift: Not Choosing at All

God did not say, “If you eat, I will kill you.”
He said, “If you eat, you will die”—that is, you will come to know death.

This is not pun­ish­ment.
It is con­sequence.

The tree was not a trap.
It was not a test of obed­i­ence.

It was pos­sib­il­ity.

And the pos­sib­il­ity was not only “Eat or do not eat.”
The pos­sib­il­ity was also to stand and see.

Not to choose at all.
Not to be swept away either by fear of the pro­hib­i­tion or by the desire to possess.
To remain there, between the two con­di­tions, and look.

This choice was true freedom.

But this was never taught.

Because choos­ing not to enter the game of power, pro­hib­i­tion and trans­gres­sion
is the greatest freedom.

And if people knew it,
there would be no control over them.

Control through False Choice

The system does not want free people.
It wants people who believe they are free while moving within a con­trolled struc­ture.

This is why everything op­er­ates within a binary:
Good or evil, left or right, obed­i­ence or dis­obedi­ence, belief or un­be­lief.

But this binary is false.

The real ques­tion is not which of the two choices you will make.
The real ques­tion is whether you will agree to play the game.

Odys­seus knew this.
That is why he told the Cyclops, “I am Nobody.”

Because when you refuse the binary, you become truly in­vis­ible to the mech­an­ism.
It cannot entrap you.
It cannot condemn you, because you have not ac­cep­ted the frame­work that would make you guilty.

This was the hidden know­ledge of the Garden.
This was the truth that had to remain hidden.

And for this reason,
when Christ came,
they had to make him unique, sep­ar­ate, ab­so­lute and un­touch­able.

Christ and His Childhood: Why Do They Not Speak of It?

Have you wondered:
Why do they not speak about Christ’s child­hood?

Why is the only story we are given his birth and then, sud­denly, his adult­hood?

Because if Christ had been re­cor­ded as a child,
it would have been obvious that he was like every child.

It would have become im­possible to main­tain that he alone was divine, the only im­macu­late infant.

If they saw di­vin­ity within every child,
they could not speak of ori­gin­al sin.
They could not make hu­man­ity feel guilty from birth.

But there had to be a sep­ar­a­tion.
There had to be the one Christ, while every­one else was born guilty.

Not because it was true.
But because without guilt, there is no need for au­thor­ity.

Divine and Human: Created and Uncreated

Christ was not sep­ar­ated from the human being.
He did not come to stand op­pos­ite hu­man­ity as some­thing it could never reach.

He was Created and Un­cre­ated.

  • Created, because he lived in the world, in the body, in time.
  • Un­cre­ated, because Spirit does not belong to lim­it­a­tions.

But this is not only true of Christ.
This is also true of every human being.

Every human being is sim­ul­tan­eously within time and beyond it.
Every human being has a created body, but also a spirit that cannot be im­prisoned.

This is why the final truth is not the choice between good and evil.
The final truth is to stand outside the binary and see.

The Restoration of Revelation

There is no ori­gin­al sin.
There is no in­her­ited guilt.
There is no human nature born in mud that must be pur­i­fied through pain.

If there is any­thing,
it is the truth they con­cealed: the ab­so­lute freedom of ex­ist­ence.

The Divine does not govern by im­pos­i­tion.
The Divine governs through care.

Hu­man­ity is not a suc­ces­sion of guilty beings await­ing re­demp­tion.
Hu­man­ity is already part of the divine; the know­ledge of this was simply con­cealed from it.

And rev­el­a­tion is not pun­ish­ment.
It is the gaze that sees without fear.

Whoever calls you to choose between one thing and another
con­ceals from you the greatest choice of all:
To choose nothing and stand so that you may see.

Because only when no one pushes you to choose a side
are you truly free.
