The Neigh­bour

I did not know that I myself could be the Neigh­bour.

I be­lieved that near­ness was the other person ap­proach­ing.

Yet who can be closer to me than I am?

How can I live while search­ing for the move­ment towards me,

if I have not already taken my stand within myself, ready to receive what comes?

And now, de­vo­tion.

Not as ob­lig­a­tion,

not as a bur­den­some com­mit­ment,

but as af­firm­a­tion.

The as­sim­il­a­tion of meaning is not de­vo­tion to meaning.

It is not the same to hold some­thing within you and to be within it.

It is not the same to un­der­stand some­thing and to give your­self to it.

The dur­a­tion of etern­ity is lived ex­per­i­ence, not time.

The eternal does not come after the tran­si­ent –

the eternal is within the tran­si­ent.

The dead person is not per­petu­ated.

He does not become con­tinu­ity through the se­quence of events.

He becomes pres­ence,

becomes some­thing that is, without needing to con­tin­ue.

He does not remain. He is.

The ul­ti­mate is neither space nor time – it is de­vo­tion.

Not some­thing that ends, but some­thing that is ful­filled.

Not some­thing that remains, but some­thing that never ceased to be.

And now, Xuthus.

Epi­daur­us finds relief in the truth that was not spoken.

Yet truth is not some­thing that re­lieves – it is some­thing that fulfils.

Who finds relief, if someone else remains in a lie?

How can history find its still­ness if some­thing within it remains rest­less?

The ques­tion is not whether one can bear it.

The ques­tion is not whether it fits within the logic of the world.

The ques­tion is whether it pleases.

I saw you, Greek.

Not as someone who belongs to a country,

not as one born upon a line of history.

But as someone who became a line.

As someone who became a page.

The page is what you turn,

it is what makes an imprint,

it is what remains without being fixed.

The page is not merely some­thing that exists – it is some­thing that bears.

The page is not merely some­thing that is written – it is some­thing that sees.

And now I know:

I am not someone who merely waits for others to draw near.

I myself am the Neigh­bour.

There is no other who must find me.

I am the one who stands, who becomes a place, who is already here.

Now, I know how to be present.
