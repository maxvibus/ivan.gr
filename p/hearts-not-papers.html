Those who answer me have hearts, not papers

Published on November 18, 2026 · 3 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

These past weeks I have asked for a great deal, and all of it in writing, because that is how I speak. I asked for ex­plan­a­tions, doc­u­ments, a refund, an account. I also asked for subtler things: for someone to hear me about a work, about an old paper, about a story I carry.

At some point I un­der­stood that I need not wait for the answers to know whom they will come from. It is enough to see what an­swer­ing would cost them.

A museum in America for­war­ded my letter the same day. A spe­cial­ist in France, who did not know me, replied the next. Two years ago I had asked an an­ti­quar­i­an book­seller about a his­tor­ic­al letter. He replied form­ally that it had been sold. I wrote to him again now, and he sent me a copy of it without a single word more. I had not asked for it that way, and it was not his ob­lig­a­tion. None of them had any­thing to protect from me.

Wherever I asked for papers that would show some­thing about the one who holds them, I found silence. Or some­thing worse: auto­mat­ic mes­sages, a phone call in place of a written reply, an “I have no voice, I am ill” to someone who never asked to hear a voice.

Among them there are also those who simply do not care, because to them a cus­tom­er is a pro­ced­ure. My printer-scanner, with which I have been di­git­ising for months the archives of entire lives, broke down under war­ranty. A war­ranty means free repair or re­place­ment, and if neither is pos­sible, a refund. Store credit, only if you choose it. The shop wrote to me that “store credit has been ap­proved”. As if I had asked a favour, and as if it could decide where I would spend my own money. It gave me its own choice, not the one the law provides for me too.

An outside build­ing manager seems to have made a mess of everything, and perhaps not by ac­ci­dent. Res­id­ents keep silent. Banks play with words, so that you get tangled in the dispute and not in the sub­stance. Even when each written re­sponse of theirs looks like an answer, they do not say what they were asked. And that is silence too.

There is another case as well. A neigh­bour in Athens asked me, “would you like to join the group with the other neigh­bours?”, added me, and fell silent with every­one else when I spoke to every­one. An­swer­ing one person costs nothing. It becomes silence when he too has some­thing to say in front of the others.

And then it was my turn. I was already so tired of shops, banks, offices, re­l­at­ives, that when a neigh­bour in the village asked my mother “how is Ivan?”, I did not hear a ques­tion. I heard an ac­cus­a­tion de­mand­ing a defence. I wrote to him in anger and loaded him with old things that were not his. He called me at once. I did not pick up. I no longer answer the phone. He wrote to me, I ex­plained, I asked his for­give­ness. In the end he wrote some­thing every person in the world has said:

“You can always count on me, whatever the hour.”

I did not reply. I know he is a human being. And every human being hurts.
