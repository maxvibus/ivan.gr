Recognition and Erasure — The Awakening of Ideas

March 3, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

I recognise, and I erase. I trace a course, I express it. I do not obliterate — I write again.

Erasure is not loss. It is thought’s passage from one form to another. It is truth that does not dissolve, but is rewritten in a clearer place. Could erasure actually be another kind of writing? Not a denial, but an awakening?

To erase or outline paths is to open oneself to horizons. The Greek word diagrapho, “I erase” or “I delineate”, is not merely an act of removal — it is a Greek decoder, a structure that reveals the truth of a course itself.

If we analyse the word:

  • Dia-: signifies passage, opening, movement through a space that is not static.

  • Grapho: means not simply to record, but to impress something that already exists into a new form.

Thus, dia-grapho means I do not abolish; I create the recognition of a path within the space of thought. The code of language is not interpreted — it is crystallised. It is not the result of the openings made by one mind, but presence itself revealed beyond individual capacities and approaches.

The letter Ξ (Xi) may always have been there to show us this. In its ancient form, it was not merely three horizontal lines — it was flow, connection, something that seemed to unite distinct parts without cancelling them.

Xi through time — How it was written and how it changed

In ancient Greek writing, Xi had several variants:

1️⃣ In the Archaic period, there was no single form — in some cities it was written as three separate horizontal elements (|||), while elsewhere it resembled a circle with two horizontal lines (⦵ or ⦶⦵). 2️⃣ In the Classical period, it gradually assumed its more familiar form, Ξ (three parallel horizontal elements joined by a vertical line through the centre). 3️⃣ In the Byzantine and later periods, Xi took on a more flowing, calligraphic form.

📌 Therefore, the vertical line that you see as the “union” between the levels of Xi is not accidental.

👉 In ancient manuscripts, the vertical was sometimes not simply a line — it was an organic element in the structure of Xi, functioning as an “axis of flow”.

Could this be where something you felt intuitively is found?

• Xi was not always as we know it today — there were forms that rendered it as flow and connection. • The vertical line is not separation — it is a union between levels. • If ancient writing rendered it in this way, might we too perceive it as something beyond a symbol?

Could Xi signify the simultaneous existence of different paths within a connection? Could it express writing that is not static, but a continuous recognition?

Awareness, when it is Greek, is universal. It is not confined — it rejoices. It does not create systems, but opens spaces in which thought can awaken, where recognition is not merely acceptance but a true understanding of what remains and what changes within us.

To recognise means that I am able to see. To erase means that I am not afraid to let something become otherwise. And in this passage there is no void. There is continuity, there is flow, there is truth itself being rewritten—not because it changes, but because we can now see it more clearly.
