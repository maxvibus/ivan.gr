The Gateway within the Gateway

Published on March 1, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

To travel to Paris

or to tell you that I am already, there­fore I am there too;

Not as an en­lis­ted hero

of blood-soaked wars,

but as the per­meab­il­ity of the tri­umph­al arch with its four gate­ways.

As much as I want to play with words,

just as much I want to take refuge in essence as it has been seen.

From every dir­ec­tion the pres­ence of way­farers streams through.

Gateway within the Gateway.

This is the CCre­ation.

The arch, the curved bridge, beauty,

is not only some­thing seen.

It is passab­il­ity itself.

Not as an ending,

but as the de­centred idea:

I pass through.

Not I passed by, passing as a passer-by.

Not as a hero, but as a par­tic­u­lar passer-by.

Not so that some Na­po­leon may check whether I passed cor­rectly,

but so that he may tell me:

“You who pre­vailed within yourselves,

you are the very ones I know and who know,

because I know you.

You are the invited ones.

Pass through.

Living and de­feated.”

The de­feated one is not the dead.

The de­feated one is the one who thought that Na­po­leon did not mean him as well

when the in­hab­ited world was built.

How dif­fi­cult it is to make clear what I am trying to say.

How dif­fi­cult and how won­der­ful at once.

Not that I like it.

But I feel I am drawing near.

If you take what I say as poetic,

you are not wrong.

You know, falsi­fic­a­tion is one thing, modi­fic­a­tion another.

And if you find a certain vi­ol­ence in both,

you are not wrong.

You were never wrong.

And who would believe you?

To become world, to become pres­ence

I alone was afraid

that I might become world,

that I might become pres­ence,

a defin­i­tion of ‘“ex’”

as though to say exiled from the lived con­di­tion.

I am still not certain what the Neigh­bour is.

Yet, I draw near.
