Spheres of Influence: The Quiet Encounter of I and You

Two spheres rest gently beside one another. Nothing on them suggests a face, an expression, an emotion. Yet it is impossible to look at them without feeling that these spheres are the I and the You.

From a psychological perspective, the two spheres symbolise healthy interdependence: the ability to coexist independently, maintaining their integrity without depending on one another for their identity. This form of presence expresses the profound human need for contact and recognition without losing individuality or autonomy.

Ontologically, the two spheres represent pure existence beyond any property or characteristic. Each exists in its own right as a self-subsisting entity, yet their presence side by side expresses the ontological fact that no existence can be conceived as absolutely alone. Their coexistence is not a relationship of dependence, but an encounter that affirms each sphere's existence through the silent, discreet recognition of the other.

The concepts of father, mother and child often refer to particular social roles and identities shaped by culture and history. Yet these concepts go beyond a narrow understanding of roles. Father and mother are not merely functions within the family; above all, they express deeper relationships of presence and acceptance. The child, too, is not merely a role required to meet certain expectations, but an existence that shapes and is shaped through authentic encounters with others.

Another sphere can be seen as your own presence as a viewer. You are this closeness, the point where the I and the You meet and become perceptible. This additional sphere symbolises your active participation, your presence as a connecting element, that which makes closeness and encounter possible and real.

This encounter is more than simple proximity. It is the moment when you and I realise that our presence, our gaze, our mutual recognition and acceptance are what allow the spheres to exist more fully. Within the gaze between us lies a deeper meaning, a silent, lucid understanding that needs no words. It is the presence that affirms our shared existence.

Here lies the transcendent leap:

There is no need for recognition, explanation or justification. The roles of father, mother and child can be transcended in a pure, quiet presence. The spheres exist without needing someone to name their relationship. They exist because they are there—simply, naturally, calmly. And you, as an additional sphere, are closeness itself, the point at which the relationship between the other spheres becomes perceptible and real.

At this point of pure presence, the I and the You are no longer separate existences, but one and the same meeting point. Their relationship is not one of tension or need. It is a relationship of absolute, lucid closeness. And in this relationship, the human gaze ceases to search for meanings and stories. It perceives something deeper: the pure, transcendent presence of the other, right beside it, with no need for anything more to be said.
