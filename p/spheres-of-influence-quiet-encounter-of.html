Spheres of Influence: The Quiet Encounter of I and You

Published on March 20, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

Two spheres rest gently beside one another. Nothing on them sug­gests a face, an ex­pres­sion, an emotion. Yet it is im­possible to look at them without feeling that these spheres are the I and the You.

From a psy­cho­lo­gic­al per­spect­ive, the two spheres sym­bol­ise healthy in­ter­de­pend­ence: the ability to coexist in­de­pend­ently, main­tain­ing their in­teg­rity without de­pend­ing on one another for their iden­tity. This form of pres­ence ex­presses the pro­found human need for contact and re­cog­ni­tion without losing in­di­vidu­al­ity or autonomy.

On­to­lo­gic­ally, the two spheres rep­res­ent pure ex­ist­ence beyond any prop­erty or char­ac­ter­ist­ic. Each exists in its own right as a self-sub­sist­ing entity, yet their pres­ence side by side ex­presses the on­to­lo­gic­al fact that no ex­ist­ence can be con­ceived as ab­so­lutely alone. Their co­ex­ist­ence is not a re­la­tion­ship of de­pend­ence, but an en­counter that affirms each sphere's ex­ist­ence through the silent, dis­creet re­cog­ni­tion of the other.

The con­cepts of father, mother and child often refer to par­tic­u­lar social roles and iden­tit­ies shaped by culture and history. Yet these con­cepts go beyond a narrow un­der­stand­ing of roles. Father and mother are not merely func­tions within the family; above all, they express deeper re­la­tion­ships of pres­ence and ac­cept­ance. The child, too, is not merely a role re­quired to meet certain ex­pect­a­tions, but an ex­ist­ence that shapes and is shaped through au­then­t­ic en­coun­ters with others.

Another sphere can be seen as your own pres­ence as a viewer. You are this close­ness, the point where the I and the You meet and become per­cept­ible. This ad­di­tion­al sphere sym­bol­ises your active par­ti­cip­a­tion, your pres­ence as a con­nect­ing element, that which makes close­ness and en­counter pos­sible and real.

This en­counter is more than simple prox­im­ity. It is the moment when you and I realise that our pres­ence, our gaze, our mutual re­cog­ni­tion and ac­cept­ance are what allow the spheres to exist more fully. Within the gaze between us lies a deeper meaning, a silent, lucid un­der­stand­ing that needs no words. It is the pres­ence that affirms our shared ex­ist­ence.

Here lies the tran­scend­ent leap:

There is no need for re­cog­ni­tion, ex­plan­a­tion or jus­ti­fic­a­tion. The roles of father, mother and child can be tran­scen­ded in a pure, quiet pres­ence. The spheres exist without needing someone to name their re­la­tion­ship. They exist because they are there—simply, nat­ur­ally, calmly. And you, as an ad­di­tion­al sphere, are close­ness itself, the point at which the re­la­tion­ship between the other spheres becomes per­cept­ible and real.

At this point of pure pres­ence, the I and the You are no longer sep­ar­ate ex­ist­ences, but one and the same meeting point. Their re­la­tion­ship is not one of tension or need. It is a re­la­tion­ship of ab­so­lute, lucid close­ness. And in this re­la­tion­ship, the human gaze ceases to search for mean­ings and stories. It per­ceives some­thing deeper: the pure, tran­scend­ent pres­ence of the other, right beside it, with no need for any­thing more to be said.
