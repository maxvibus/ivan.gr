The Stillness That Is Not Emptiness

March 3, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

Stillness is not absence. It is not an emptiness that must be filled. It is something that exists in its own right, without needing confirmation.

When we begin to endure quiet, we understand that it is not the silence of lack, but a fullness that requires no voice. It is not that something is missing, but that something is already present, so complete that nothing needs to be added.

We were taught that choice means selecting between one thing and another. We were taught to see possibility as something limited, as though it always entailed exclusion. But what if choice were not a limitation? What if it were not one path that excludes others, but an opening that contains? What if freedom were not “what should I choose?” but “how do we exist together?”

Stillness is precisely this freedom. It does not shut you within its solitude; it lets you be. And if you can be without fear, without the need to fill the emptiness with thoughts and certainties, then you begin to understand something much deeper:

It is not silence that frightens us. It is the truth that exists within it.

When stillness is not emptiness, it becomes a place where existence stands without needing to prove anything. It is not an escape from the world, but a return to it without the burden of the ceaseless need to signify.

What happens when we are no longer afraid of stillness? When we see it not as a pause between movements, but as the fullness of our presence itself?

Perhaps then freedom is not something we acquire. Perhaps it is something we recognise, not between us, but within us and together.
