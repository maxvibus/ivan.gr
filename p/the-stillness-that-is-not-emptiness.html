The Stillness That Is Not Emptiness

March 3, 2025 · 1 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

Still­ness is not absence. It is not an empti­ness that must be filled. It is some­thing that exists in its own right, without needing con­firm­a­tion.

When we begin to endure quiet, we un­der­stand that it is not the silence of lack, but a full­ness that re­quires no voice. It is not that some­thing is missing, but that some­thing is already present, so com­plete that nothing needs to be added.

We were taught that choice means se­lect­ing between one thing and another. We were taught to see pos­sib­il­ity as some­thing limited, as though it always en­tailed ex­clu­sion. But what if choice were not a lim­it­a­tion? What if it were not one path that ex­cludes others, but an opening that con­tains? What if freedom were not “what should I choose?” but “how do we exist to­geth­er?”

Still­ness is pre­cisely this freedom. It does not shut you within its solitude; it lets you be. And if you can be without fear, without the need to fill the empti­ness with thoughts and cer­tain­ties, then you begin to un­der­stand some­thing much deeper:

It is not silence that fright­ens us. It is the truth that exists within it.

When still­ness is not empti­ness, it becomes a place where ex­ist­ence stands without needing to prove any­thing. It is not an escape from the world, but a return to it without the burden of the cease­less need to signify.

What happens when we are no longer afraid of still­ness? When we see it not as a pause between move­ments, but as the full­ness of our pres­ence itself?

Perhaps then freedom is not some­thing we acquire. Perhaps it is some­thing we re­cog­nise, not between us, but within us and to­geth­er.
