Two kinds of people

Published on March 13, 2026 · 1 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

Tech­no­logy always creates two kinds of people: those who use it to sim­pli­fy and those who use it to go deeper.

Print­ing gave us both cheap sen­sa­tion­al pub­lic­a­tions and Shakespeare. The in­ter­net gave us both viral content and new forms of philo­sophy.

LLMs (Large Lan­guage Models) will give us both people who will never need to think again — and people who will think more deeply than was ever pos­sible before.

Yuval Noah Harari speaks of a divide between people with and without access to in­tel­li­gence tech­no­logy. But he sees it as eco­nom­ic and polit­ic­al in­equal­ity — not as a dif­fer­ence in how thought is pro­duced.

In The Shal­lows, Nick Carr said that the in­ter­net makes think­ing su­per­fi­cial. But he did not see the op­pos­ite pos­sib­il­ity.

No one has said that the same tech­no­logy will sim­ul­tan­eously create the most su­per­fi­cial and the deepest think­ing that has ever existed. And that the dis­tance between them will be the de­fin­ing feature of the civil­isa­tion to come.
