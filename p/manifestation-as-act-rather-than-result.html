Manifestation as Act Rather than Result: Present Revelation as Embodied Dwelling Together

Published on July 12, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

When people hear the word Rev­el­a­tion, they usually think of a future filled with the end of the world, judge­ment, heav­enly voices and final acts. In the un­con­scious of re­li­gious edu­ca­tion, rev­el­a­tion is often the “moment of truth” that comes af­ter­wards, as pun­ish­ment or triumph. Yet this view already arrives too late, because it un­der­stands mani­fest­a­tion as a result: some­thing that will come, some­thing that will be re­vealed, rather than as the very act of pres­ence.

The theo­logy of mani­fest­a­tion, when united with em­bod­ied life, is not the ex­pect­a­tion of a spec­tacle. It is the re­cog­ni­tion that God does not hide in order to be re­vealed, but remains in order to emerge. Not as an in­ter­ven­tion in a cosmic clock, but as a lived ex­per­i­ence already moving along a path of trust: in sight, in flesh, in empathy, in near­ness.

Thus, Rev­el­a­tion is not a moment in history but the call of the person within history. It is not a volume of the future but the very voice of re­la­tion­ship saying: “I am here.” This is why every en­counter re­cog­nised as mutual—that is, not ma­nip­u­lat­ive or com­pet­it­ive, but friendly, dia­lo­gic­al and true—is already a mani­fest­a­tion.

Here, too, the problem of ex­pect­a­tion arises: many people miss the mani­fest­a­tion because they are waiting for it. They live “with their eyes on heaven” rather than on the person before them. They do not see the in­hab­ited world, the other, the moment, as a mani­fest­a­tion of God, because they are waiting for some greater miracle. Yet this is the depth of the mystery: the greatest miracle is not some­thing greater, but some­thing here.

Mani­fest­a­tion is not merely theo­lo­gic­al. It is also cos­mo­lo­gic­al, phys­ic­al, psy­cho­lo­gic­al and ex­ist­en­tial. When you open your heart to someone, when the gaze re­cog­nises the other not in terms of fear or com­pet­i­tion but of dwell­ing to­geth­er, then Rev­el­a­tion has already happened.

And then, logos is no longer in­form­a­tion. It is light. It is a shift in the mode, not only in the meaning. And this is why mani­fest­a­tion is not written down as an event; it becomes a way of in­hab­it­ing meaning.
