Manifestation as Act Rather than Result: Present Revelation as Embodied Dwelling Together

July 12, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

When people hear the word Revelation, they usually think of a future filled with the end of the world, judgement, heavenly voices and final acts. In the unconscious of religious education, revelation is often the “moment of truth” that comes afterwards, as punishment or triumph. Yet this view already arrives too late, because it understands manifestation as a result: something that will come, something that will be revealed, rather than as the very act of presence.

The theology of manifestation, when united with embodied life, is not the expectation of a spectacle. It is the recognition that God does not hide in order to be revealed, but remains in order to emerge. Not as an intervention in a cosmic clock, but as a lived experience already moving along a path of trust: in sight, in flesh, in empathy, in nearness.

Thus, Revelation is not a moment in history but the call of the person within history. It is not a volume of the future but the very voice of relationship saying: “I am here.” This is why every encounter recognised as mutual—that is, not manipulative or competitive, but friendly, dialogical and true—is already a manifestation.

Here, too, the problem of expectation arises: many people miss the manifestation because they are waiting for it. They live “with their eyes on heaven” rather than on the person before them. They do not see the inhabited world, the other, the moment, as a manifestation of God, because they are waiting for some greater miracle. Yet this is the depth of the mystery: the greatest miracle is not something greater, but something here.

Manifestation is not merely theological. It is also cosmological, physical, psychological and existential. When you open your heart to someone, when the gaze recognises the other not in terms of fear or competition but of dwelling together, then Revelation has already happened.

And then, logos is no longer information. It is light. It is a shift in the mode, not only in the meaning. And this is why manifestation is not written down as an event; it becomes a way of inhabiting meaning.
