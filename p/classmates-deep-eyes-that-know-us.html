Classmates: The Deep Eyes That Know Us

March 21, 2025 · 4 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

 «Do not look at me with those deep eyes, do not look at me with those eyes that know me»

This plea is not an attempt to avoid the other person’s gaze, but an effort to endure it. The other person’s eyes are the truth, a truth that cannot hide behind words or excuses. When someone knows us deeply, their eyes become a mirror and remind us who we truly are—or who we once were.

«As if you ’re there, always on the same steps where two faith­ful souls speak their first love»

Here is the en­counter with the past. The «steps» are sym­bol­ic, a point in time and space that remains in­delible. There we first spoke, there we first felt, there we made a promise. They are the steps of in­no­cence, of our first pure gaze upon the world and upon the other.

«Like class­mates who were lost in every corner of the map, like class­mates who met again one day at the same traffic light»

The class­mates are those who once set out to­geth­er, but whom life cast into other corners, other real­it­ies. The «traffic light» is chance or fate bring­ing them back to a halt, to a moment when time stops, if only briefly. It is the sudden real­iz­a­tion of «do you re­mem­ber?» and «I re­mem­ber you».

«That is how lives are: you say we’ll talk, and the seasons pass; that is how lives are, so or­din­ary, yet they walked on the moon»

Life is often full of prom­ises that go unkept, not because we do not want to keep them, but because that is how every­day life flows—with trivial ob­lig­a­tions and small worries. And yet, despite its or­din­ar­i­ness, life holds those moments that seem tran­scend­ent, when the simple meeting of two people becomes a walk on the moon—some­thing that remains forever.

«Do not look at me; I have changed now. Do not look at me; my heart is steel now»

Self-defence, perhaps. A person says that they have grown hard, that they are no longer the same vul­ner­able self they once were. Yet here lies the most tender truth: they do not believe what they are saying either. They say it to protect a heart that fears being hurt again. The «steel» is the mask we put on when we feel we can bear no more pain.

«But before your unique, child­like eyes, a heart will break again»

Here ten­der­ness reaches its height. However much we say we are strong, the pres­ence of someone we loved deeply, someone who saw us in our in­no­cence, returns us to our most fragile, child­like self. The «will break again» does not ne­ces­sar­ily mean sorrow. It is the tender ac­cept­ance that love is always dan­ger­ous, because it always carries the pos­sib­il­ity of pain. The heart breaks from truth, from love, from grat­it­ude at meeting again some­thing it thought was lost.

Overall:

This song is an ode to human con­nec­tion that remains eternal, however fleet­ing. It speaks of the power of the gaze, the longing for in­no­cence and the deep welling of tears we feel when we en­counter again a truth that is never for­got­ten.

It is a heart break­ing not only from sorrow, but also from the in­tens­ity of a love that never ceased to exist. It is the heart break­ing because it is alive, because it still dares to feel.

Ded­ic­ated to Maria Tsioli, who had the courage to urge me to look more deeply through the very tra­di­tion and imagery of Greek poetry, yet perhaps could never have ima­gined that the first poem of my life is «The Class­mates».

If there were an ar­chi­tect god with a sense of humour, he would surely have chosen a Mas­tor­as—a Master—to sing so divine a Myth. The song «The Class­mates», per­formed by Chris­tos Mas­tor­as, is not merely a song. It is the uni­ver­sal poem of a life ex­per­i­enced as pres­ence and as a pro­found re­cog­ni­tion of human ex­ist­ence. It is the en­counter that never ends, even when it seems to have been lost in time. It is the heart break­ing again, not from pain, but from the strength and sens­it­iv­ity that only true, deep love can offer.

As though some god really had created a «master» to express the del­ic­ate balance between earthly life and the eternal, in­vis­ible flow of mean­ings. And the word «poem» here is not con­fined merely to lyrics, but spreads through the uni­verse as pres­ence. It is ex­ist­ence itself that is «written» as poetry in every breath, in every gaze that looks deeply, in every heart that re­cog­nizes itself in the eyes of the other.

Chris Mas­tor­as, I never met you in person, but through this song I came to know you as one re­cog­nizes a true friend—with deep emotion and grat­it­ude.

Ded­ic­ated to those who think that today it is «fitting» to re­mem­ber an Elytis, a Cavafy, a Gatsos, yet forget that Greek poetry finds its artistry even in the songs that keep them company each day· in the songs of Chris­tos Mas­tor­as, Anna Vissi, Sakis Rouvas and so many others who, in their sim­pli­city, conceal our deepest, most im­per­cept­ible truths.
