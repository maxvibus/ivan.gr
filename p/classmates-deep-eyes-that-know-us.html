Classmates: The Deep Eyes That Know Us

March 21, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

 «Do not look at me with those deep eyes, do not look at me with those eyes that know me»

This plea is not an attempt to avoid the other person’s gaze, but an effort to endure it. The other person’s eyes are the truth, a truth that cannot hide behind words or excuses. When someone knows us deeply, their eyes become a mirror and remind us who we truly are—or who we once were.

«As if you ’re there, always on the same steps where two faithful souls speak their first love»

Here is the encounter with the past. The «steps» are symbolic, a point in time and space that remains indelible. There we first spoke, there we first felt, there we made a promise. They are the steps of innocence, of our first pure gaze upon the world and upon the other.

«Like classmates who were lost in every corner of the map, like classmates who met again one day at the same traffic light»

The classmates are those who once set out together, but whom life cast into other corners, other realities. The «traffic light» is chance or fate bringing them back to a halt, to a moment when time stops, if only briefly. It is the sudden realization of «do you remember?» and «I remember you».

«That is how lives are: you say we’ll talk, and the seasons pass; that is how lives are, so ordinary, yet they walked on the moon»

Life is often full of promises that go unkept, not because we do not want to keep them, but because that is how everyday life flows—with trivial obligations and small worries. And yet, despite its ordinariness, life holds those moments that seem transcendent, when the simple meeting of two people becomes a walk on the moon—something that remains forever.

«Do not look at me; I have changed now. Do not look at me; my heart is steel now»

Self-defence, perhaps. A person says that they have grown hard, that they are no longer the same vulnerable self they once were. Yet here lies the most tender truth: they do not believe what they are saying either. They say it to protect a heart that fears being hurt again. The «steel» is the mask we put on when we feel we can bear no more pain.

«But before your unique, childlike eyes, a heart will break again»

Here tenderness reaches its height. However much we say we are strong, the presence of someone we loved deeply, someone who saw us in our innocence, returns us to our most fragile, childlike self. The «will break again» does not necessarily mean sorrow. It is the tender acceptance that love is always dangerous, because it always carries the possibility of pain. The heart breaks from truth, from love, from gratitude at meeting again something it thought was lost.

Overall:

This song is an ode to human connection that remains eternal, however fleeting. It speaks of the power of the gaze, the longing for innocence and the deep welling of tears we feel when we encounter again a truth that is never forgotten.

It is a heart breaking not only from sorrow, but also from the intensity of a love that never ceased to exist. It is the heart breaking because it is alive, because it still dares to feel.

Dedicated to Maria Tsioli, who had the courage to urge me to look more deeply through the very tradition and imagery of Greek poetry, yet perhaps could never have imagined that the first poem of my life is «The Classmates».

If there were an architect god with a sense of humour, he would surely have chosen a Mastoras—a Master—to sing so divine a Myth. The song «The Classmates», performed by Christos Mastoras, is not merely a song. It is the universal poem of a life experienced as presence and as a profound recognition of human existence. It is the encounter that never ends, even when it seems to have been lost in time. It is the heart breaking again, not from pain, but from the strength and sensitivity that only true, deep love can offer.

As though some god really had created a «master» to express the delicate balance between earthly life and the eternal, invisible flow of meanings. And the word «poem» here is not confined merely to lyrics, but spreads through the universe as presence. It is existence itself that is «written» as poetry in every breath, in every gaze that looks deeply, in every heart that recognizes itself in the eyes of the other.

Chris Mastoras, I never met you in person, but through this song I came to know you as one recognizes a true friend—with deep emotion and gratitude.
