When wonder wonders about itself

March 6, 2026

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

I think that by keeping a certain distance, I do not cut myself off from what happens around me. Events are not life itself, nor, of course, are they independent of it. In short, I keep observing observation.

In the past, the time I gave to attention was drawn like a magnet towards events; usually with puzzlement, wonder, anger or disappointment.

Recently, however, the reflection that I exist in the world was followed by another: that I also exist for my thought itself. There I found a distance that gave me respite from people’s inclinations — whether to speak emptily, to admonish, or unconsciously to cry out their emptiness.

People’s actions and reactions happen as they always have and as they will continue to happen, I thought. But who am I, observing them? What am I to myself?

Recently I heard someone say, “You are not your name.” It startled me. I wondered. I found something familiar and at the same time liberating. And a question gently awoke within me: who am I when I wonder about something?

Not who I am in general. Not what I identify with. Not which way I lean or do not lean.

But this:
what is it that wonders within me?
What is this manifestation of wonder, beyond its content?

Consciousness.

I wonder at the fact that I wonder. I am startled as if for the first time by something that is, on the one hand, new within me and, on the other, something I recognise as an already familiar trait: being naturally startled by wonder itself — wondering in amazement at the fact that I wonder.

Perhaps here one encounters an old path of thought. Socrates spoke of wonder as the beginning of philosophy; of that moment when a person stands in wonder before what is manifest. And much later, Edmund Husserl would describe reflective consciousness: thought turning towards itself.

Perhaps, then, wonder is not merely a reaction to something we see in the world, but a subtle manifestation of consciousness itself within us — the moment when thought observes that it is thinking, or simply that it is happening, or even that it is resting within itself.
