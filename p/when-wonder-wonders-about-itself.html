When wonder wonders about itself

Published March 6, 2026 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

I think that by keeping a certain dis­tance, I do not cut myself off from what happens around me. Events are not life itself, nor, of course, are they in­de­pend­ent of it. In short, I keep ob­serving ob­ser­va­tion.

In the past, the time I gave to at­ten­tion was drawn like a magnet towards events; usually with puz­zle­ment, wonder, anger or dis­ap­point­ment.

Re­cently, however, the re­flec­tion that I exist in the world was fol­lowed by another: that I also exist for my thought itself. There I found a dis­tance that gave me respite from people’s in­clin­a­tions — whether to speak emptily, to ad­mon­ish, or un­con­sciously to cry out their empti­ness.

People’s actions and re­ac­tions happen as they always have and as they will con­tin­ue to happen, I thought. But who am I, ob­serving them? What am I to myself?

Re­cently I heard someone say, “You are not your name.” It startled me. I wondered. I found some­thing fa­mil­i­ar and at the same time lib­er­at­ing. And a ques­tion gently awoke within me: who am I when I wonder about some­thing?

Not who I am in general. Not what I identi­fy with. Not which way I lean or do not lean.

But this:
what is it that wonders within me?
What is this mani­fest­a­tion of wonder, beyond its content?

Con­scious­ness.

I wonder at the fact that I wonder. I am startled as if for the first time by some­thing that is, on the one hand, new within me and, on the other, some­thing I re­cog­nise as an already fa­mil­i­ar trait: being nat­ur­ally startled by wonder itself — won­der­ing in amazement at the fact that I wonder.

Perhaps here one en­coun­ters an old path of thought. So­crates spoke of wonder as the be­gin­ning of philo­sophy; of that moment when a person stands in wonder before what is mani­fest. And much later, Edmund Husserl would de­scribe re­flect­ive con­scious­ness: thought turning towards itself.

Perhaps, then, wonder is not merely a re­ac­tion to some­thing we see in the world, but a subtle mani­fest­a­tion of con­scious­ness itself within us — the moment when thought ob­serves that it is think­ing, or simply that it is hap­pen­ing, or even that it is resting within itself.
