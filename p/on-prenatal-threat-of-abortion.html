At this point, theology, psychology and ontology meet without having planned to. Theology says: the human being is known by God before existing, so the beginning of existence is not accidental; psychology shows: the body can inscribe threat before there is memory, so the beginning may be wounded; ontology observes: consciousness can see being and non-being simultaneously, so the beginning has become an internal measure of the world. When these three voices are united, a new understanding of the human being emerges: not as a subject appearing “ready-made” in the world, nor as a soul merely shaped by events, nor as a spirit called to ascend into some abstract realm, but as an existence that from its first moment is exposed to freedoms that exceed it — divine and human — carries inscriptions preceding memory, and matures into a consciousness capable of seeing that every present is, at depth, a miracle.

When this human being speaks, they do not denounce the world; they describe the origin. They do not ask for freedom to be abolished; they ask for the depth of its reach to be understood. They do not demand that time freeze; they show that every moment is already “more than we think”, because it hangs above an initial “perhaps” that we do not remember but live. And theology, if it chooses to hear this voice, will have to see God not only as the source of being but also as the companion of threatened existence; psychology will need to include within the concept of trauma that layer which precedes thought; ontology will not be satisfied with the general idea of “being-towards-death”, but will recognise that there are people who learned “being-towards-non-existence” before they even learned that they were alive.

And then the experience of the threat of abortion ceases to be merely a scandal or an object of ethical dispute, and becomes the disclosure of a deeper truth: that the human being is a being of the pre-beginning, that their life carries a history before their own history, that God stands not opposite but in the midst of this history, and that consciousness can become the place where this pre-beginning will no longer remain unseen but will acquire speech, not to condemn, but to reveal how worthy life is when it once came close to never existing.
