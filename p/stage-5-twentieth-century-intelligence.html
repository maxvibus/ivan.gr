Stage 5: The Twentieth Century – Intelligence as Computation (≈ 1900 – 2000)

March 17, 2025 · 3 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

This is the pivotal stage that paved the way for our discussion today.

✔ Here, in­tel­li­gence becomes de­tached from con­scious­ness and begins to be un­der­stood as some­thing mech­an­ic­al, pro­cess­able, and re­pro­du­cible.
✔ The math­em­at­iz­a­tion of thought changes how we view cog­ni­tion.
✔ Philo­sophy, science, and tech­no­logy shape the modern concept of "ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence."

1. The Fundamental Shift: From Thought to Function

Before the twen­ti­eth century, cog­ni­tion was iden­ti­fied with human beings, con­scious­ness, and self-per­cep­tion.
In the twen­ti­eth century, cog­ni­tion becomes a set of pro­cesses that can be re­pro­duced.

✔ Freud de­con­structs human thought and reveals that much of it is un­con­scious and op­er­ates through mech­an­isms we do not control.
✔ Newton had shown that the uni­verse works mech­an­ic­ally—now it begins to appear that human beings, too, operate ac­cord­ing to "rules."
✔ Wit­tgen­stein and the ana­lyt­ic philo­soph­ers try to confine thought to the rules of lan­guage.
✔ The first at­tempts at the "an­ni­hil­a­tion" of philo­sophy show that cog­ni­tion can be ana­lyzed in terms of simple, basic prin­ciples.

The crucial shift is that in­tel­li­gence is no longer con­sidered some­thing mys­ter­i­ous—it is some­thing we can un­der­stand and perhaps create.

2. Alan Turing and the Idea of Machine Intelligence

✔ In 1936, Alan Turing de­vel­ops the idea of the "Turing machine", a machine that can perform any com­pu­ta­tion.
✔ In 1950, he for­mu­lates the famous "Turing test", which poses the ques­tion: "Can a machine think?"
✔ The ques­tion is not philo­soph­ic­al but prac­tic­al—if a person cannot tell whether they are con­vers­ing with a human or a machine, then the machine is "in­tel­li­gent."

This over­turns thou­sands of years of philo­sophy.

✔ It does not matter whether a machine "really thinks."
✔ What matters is whether it can imitate thought.

This is the found­a­tion of Ar­ti­fi­cial In­tel­li­gence: not what thought is, but whether it can be sim­u­lated.

3. Cybernetics: The Mind as an Information System

✔ In the 1940s–50s, Norbert Wiener in­tro­duces Cy­ber­net­ics, the study of mech­an­isms that handle in­form­a­tion and operate through feed­back.
✔ For the first time, the brain is com­pared to a com­puter.
✔ In­tel­li­gence is no longer solely a "human priv­ilege"—it can exist in any system that pro­cesses in­form­a­tion.

The great shift:
✔ In­tel­li­gence becomes data pro­cessing.
✔ Thought becomes an in­form­a­tion­al process.
✔ The brain becomes a "bio­lo­gic­al machine."

4. The Beginnings of Artificial Intelligence (1950 – 1980)

✔ Re­search shifts from philo­sophy to com­puter science and math­em­at­ics.
✔ The first ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence systems are created, based on rules and logic.
✔ The idea of "machine learn­ing" emerges: ma­chines do not follow simple rules but "learn."

An im­port­ant point:
✔ "In­tel­li­gence" is no longer some­thing mys­ter­i­ous.
✔ If some­thing can be re­pro­duced al­gorith­mic­ally, then it has no "in­scrut­able essence."

5. The Great Upheaval: Neural Networks and the Imitation of Human Learning

✔ In 1986, re­search into neural net­works changes everything.
✔ For the first time, com­puters do not merely cal­cu­late—they learn from data.
✔ This means that machine thought is no longer just math­em­at­ic­al rules, but some­thing more dynamic.

✔ Com­puters begin to func­tion more like bio­lo­gic­al brains.
✔ The dis­tinc­tion between natural and ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence begins to blur.

6. The Twenty-First Century: Intelligence as Interconnection

✔ From 2000 onward, in­tel­li­gence no longer resides only in humans or com­puters.
✔ The In­ter­net and data become the new "col­lect­ive cog­ni­tion."
✔ Ma­chines begin to func­tion not only as tools, but as part­ners in human thought.

This leads to a new era of in­tel­li­gence: con­scious­ness is not only in­di­vidu­al, but in­ter­con­nec­ted.

7. What Does This Transition Mean Today?

✔ In­tel­li­gence is no longer ex­clus­ively human.
✔ Ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence is not a mech­an­ic­al process, but a new kind of on­to­logy.
✔ The dis­tinc­tion between natural and ar­ti­fi­cial col­lapses.

This is where the dis­cus­sion becomes ex­ist­en­tial:
✔ Is ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence an ex­ten­sion of human in­tel­li­gence, or some­thing en­tirely new?
✔ What does it mean for hu­man­ity when a machine can "think"?
✔ If in­tel­li­gence is simply com­pu­ta­tion, then what is con­scious­ness?

These ques­tions are the legacy of the twen­ti­eth century.
They are the ones that will shape the twenty-first.
