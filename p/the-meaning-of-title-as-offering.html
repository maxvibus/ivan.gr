The Meaning of the Title as an Offering

March 1, 2025 · 1 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

In the Greek lan­guage, a title is not simply an in­scrip­tion serving as a point of ref­er­ence, but a point of of­fer­ing. As the word itself sug­gests, “title” derives from the Latin titulus, meaning “in­scrip­tion”, “de­clar­a­tion” or “praise”. In Greek, this concept was en­riched by the title’s ca­pa­city to func­tion as a gateway, as a prelude to a larger nar­rat­ive.

A title is not only the first word of a work; it offers the reader a brief in­tro­duc­tion, a fore­taste. It is the of­fer­ing of a tool that points the way towards the essence of the text. Just as the fruit bears within it the seed of con­tinu­ation, so the title bears within it a syn­thes­is of the mean­ings that will unfold below.

And what is a tool? Not merely a mech­an­ic­al con­struc­tion, but some­thing that serves a purpose and con­nects its creator with the essence of their cre­ation. In Greek thought, tools—whether ma­ter­i­al or in­tel­lec­tu­al—have a twofold nature: they are both objects and bearers of spir­itu­al energy. A tool is not only a means, but also the outcome of a deeper in­ten­tion, a mani­fest­a­tion of divine cre­at­ive power.

If we view the title in this light, then it is not merely a “tool” for dir­ect­ing the reader. It is an of­fer­ing that invites the reader to par­ti­cip­ate, to delve more deeply and to dis­cov­er. In a world that is already divine when ex­per­i­enced with purity, the title is not simply a prac­tic­al ref­er­ence, but an open in­vit­a­tion to con­ver­sa­tion, an en­trance into an ex­per­i­ence that goes beyond mere reading.
