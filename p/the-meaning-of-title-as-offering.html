The Meaning of the Title as an Offering

March 1, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

In the Greek language, a title is not simply an inscription serving as a point of reference, but a point of offering. As the word itself suggests, “title” derives from the Latin titulus, meaning “inscription”, “declaration” or “praise”. In Greek, this concept was enriched by the title’s capacity to function as a gateway, as a prelude to a larger narrative.

A title is not only the first word of a work; it offers the reader a brief introduction, a foretaste. It is the offering of a tool that points the way towards the essence of the text. Just as the fruit bears within it the seed of continuation, so the title bears within it a synthesis of the meanings that will unfold below.

And what is a tool? Not merely a mechanical construction, but something that serves a purpose and connects its creator with the essence of their creation. In Greek thought, tools—whether material or intellectual—have a twofold nature: they are both objects and bearers of spiritual energy. A tool is not only a means, but also the outcome of a deeper intention, a manifestation of divine creative power.

If we view the title in this light, then it is not merely a “tool” for directing the reader. It is an offering that invites the reader to participate, to delve more deeply and to discover. In a world that is already divine when experienced with purity, the title is not simply a practical reference, but an open invitation to conversation, an entrance into an experience that goes beyond mere reading.
