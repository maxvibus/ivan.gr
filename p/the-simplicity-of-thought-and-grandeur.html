The Simplicity of Thought and the Grandeur of Experience

May 30, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

Deeper awareness is born not from simply adding knowledge, but from removing unnecessary concepts, from removing whatever obstructs clarity of vision. The essence of understanding often lies in the distance we take from the obvious, in the movement of stepping back that allows us to see the true face of things, hidden beneath their familiar surface. Every densely layered meaning invites an inner shift, a silent admission that the most important truths are not those we can readily express, but those that dwell at the edge of silence, where language yields before the grandeur of experience. Thought thus becomes an act of simplicity, an act of reduction that leads not to poverty, but to the rich freedom of clear perception. What remains, then, is not merely what we know, but what we are truly able to see and experience directly, with nothing intervening except the very clarity of presence.

One example of an unnecessary concept is the constant search for confirmation of our worth through other people’s judgments. This concept is unnecessary because it adds no real understanding of who we are. Instead, it distracts us and clouds our clear image of ourselves, obstructing the authentic experience of existence and of our actual presence in the world. Identifying such unnecessary concepts is the first act of stepping back that prepares us to grasp true understanding beyond the obvious.

True understanding often does not come from an immediate, self-evident perception of a situation. Instead, it requires a conscious movement away from what initially seems obvious or given, so that we can gain a broader perspective.

This stepping back is not avoidance, but an active movement that enables us to see the deeper and often unseen aspects of things. Beneath the surface — behind what appears at first glance — lie the essence, the truth, the deeper reality. When we take such a distance, perception expands, allowing us to discern the true face, the actual nature of situations, free from prejudice, hasty conclusions or superficial judgments.

In other words, to see clearly, we must move beyond the immediate and enter a space where our gaze is free from the limits of a first impression. From there, reality appears truer and more substantial.

The “grandeur of experience” refers to the distinctive quality of moments that exceed language’s ability to express them fully. These are experiences in which we feel we are touching something deeper, something almost inexpressible and yet profoundly real. They may be moments of pure joy, a sudden awareness, a deep connection with someone or something, or even our simple yet complete presence in the here and now.

This grandeur lies precisely in the fact that the experience is so immediate and complete that it does away with the need for description or explanation — it is absolute and self-sufficient in itself. It is like a small revelation, in which life discloses its true depth, transcending the superficiality of everyday life and reminding us that there is a level of existence always there, ready to surprise and profoundly move us.

Thought as an act of simplicity means that substantial, deep thinking does not arise from accumulating ever more information or ideas, but from selectively removing what is unnecessary, superfluous or obstructs clear perception. This simplicity is a process of clearing the mind: instead of continually adding new layers of knowledge, thought removes whatever does not lead to clarity and lucidity. Space is thus freed within us to perceive truths that are simple but not simplistic. This simplicity helps us experience thought as pure presence, as lucidity, as immediate experience, rather than merely as intellectual overload.

In this way, thought as an act of simplicity leads not to intellectual poverty, but to a wealth of clear and substantial awareness. It is a practice that leads to calm, to authenticity and, ultimately, to a deeper and more authentic relationship with ourselves and the world around us.
