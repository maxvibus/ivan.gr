Between the Human and the Divine

Published March 8, 2025 · 1 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original


There is a place where the human and the divine are not two dif­fer­ent things, but one and the same, in two states of aware­ness. The person who knows sees. The person who does not know waits. And while waiting, they place the divine far away, outside them­selves, outside every­day life, outside the sim­pli­city of pres­ence.

The in-between is not dis­tance. It is un­aware­ness of what already exists. The human is already divine, but human beings have for­got­ten this. And because they have for­got­ten, they seek it as some­thing outside them­selves.

They do not re­cog­nize them­selves as divine, because they learned to see the divine as some­thing greater, some­thing de­mand­ing worship, some­thing beyond per­ishab­il­ity, beyond the body, beyond the world.

But the divine is not far away. It is the very gaze that holds steady and does not turn away.

When someone looks at you and sees not merely your form, but your ex­ist­ence—there is the divine. When a person stands before the grandeur of the world and does not bow their head in fear—there is the divine.

The human and the divine are not sep­ar­ated by dis­tance. They are sep­ar­ated by a for­get­ting—the amnesia of their own truth.

If they re­membered, they would not pray in waiting. They would pray as already ex­ist­ing within it. They would live in a way that asked no ques­tions, but was itself the answer.

And then the in-between would cease to exist. There would be nothing to bridge. Because the divine was already here. And the human was already whole.
