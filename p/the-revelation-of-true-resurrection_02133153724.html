The Revelation of True Resurrection: From Spirit to the Repudiation of Violence

March 1, 2025 · 3 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

Re­sur­rec­tion, in its prim­or­di­al meaning, denotes raising up: the el­ev­a­tion and res­tor­a­tion of the spirit and of ex­ist­ence. It is an inward and spir­itu­al process, a re­cov­ery from a fall—not ne­ces­sar­ily bio­lo­gic­al or phys­ic­al, but more es­sen­tial and ex­ist­en­tial. Over time, however, the concept of re­sur­rec­tion was trans­formed into a doc­trine about the dead rising in the future, within a distant eschat­o­lo­gic­al frame­work. This trans­form­a­tion was not ac­ci­dent­al; it was the product of a violent muta­tion of the ori­gin­al spir­itu­al teach­ings.

This vi­ol­ence came from those who, in pursuit of power, sought to impose a one-di­men­sion­al nar­rat­ive. Using words, images and doc­trines, those in au­thor­ity dis­tor­ted the meaning of re­sur­rec­tion to create fear and sub­mis­sion. They turned the spir­itu­al event of being raised up into an image of terror and ab­so­lute control: no longer the rebirth of the soul, but the ex­pect­a­tion of a moment of judge­ment when the dead would rise to be judged. This control through fear of the Second Coming and Judge­ment aimed to subdue the spirit, so that it would not seek freedom but remain con­fined within es­tab­lished beliefs.

At the same time, the phrase “New World Order” was presen­ted as a sup­posed renewal, a prom­ised rebirth of social and polit­ic­al or­gan­isa­tion. At a deeper level, however, it amounts to a sys­tem­at­ic attempt to control cul­tur­al and spir­itu­al wealth. Looking through history, we see that what was called the “New Order” became as­so­ci­ated with the es­tab­lish­ment of struc­tures that served neither truth nor cre­at­ive de­vel­op­ment, but ho­mo­gen­isa­tion, the sup­pres­sion of dif­fer­ence, and the pre­dom­in­ance of par­tic­u­lar ideo­lo­gic­al and eco­nom­ic in­terests.

In Greece, the tar­get­ing was not only geo­graph­ic­al or polit­ic­al. It was primar­ily lin­guist­ic. The Greek lan­guage, with its in­com­par­able ex­press­ive ca­pa­city, was and remains a bearer not only of culture but also of truth. Through the erosion of lan­guage, control was sought over thought, tem­por­al­ity, and the very ability of human beings to per­ceive life and their ex­ist­ence freely. This was the reason for di­vid­ing time into “BC” and “AD”, a di­vi­sion in­ten­ded not merely to cat­egor­ise his­tor­ic­al periods, but to impose a par­tic­u­lar theo­lo­gic­al and cul­tur­al per­spect­ive. In this way, an attempt was made to sever modern human beings from their roots, from the primal lan­guage and its truth, and to subject them to a man­age­able and con­trolled present.

What truly troubled them was the freedom in­spired by the Greek tra­di­tion. It was not only the culture of an­tiquity that had to be for­got­ten, but the very struc­ture of thought and ex­pres­sion. This war against lan­guage and spirit was not a simple struggle for dom­in­ance; it was an attempt to conceal the truth, to deprive human beings of the right to re­cog­nise and ex­per­i­ence the meaning of their own ex­ist­ence. And for this reason, the rev­el­at­ory concept of the coming was dis­tor­ted, not because it con­tained nothing true, but because that truth had to be buried beneath fear, di­vi­sion and the “New Order” of those who manage the spirit.
