The Revelation of True Resurrection: From Spirit to the Repudiation of Violence

March 1, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

Resurrection, in its primordial meaning, denotes raising up: the elevation and restoration of the spirit and of existence. It is an inward and spiritual process, a recovery from a fall—not necessarily biological or physical, but more essential and existential. Over time, however, the concept of resurrection was transformed into a doctrine about the dead rising in the future, within a distant eschatological framework. This transformation was not accidental; it was the product of a violent mutation of the original spiritual teachings.

This violence came from those who, in pursuit of power, sought to impose a one-dimensional narrative. Using words, images and doctrines, those in authority distorted the meaning of resurrection to create fear and submission. They turned the spiritual event of being raised up into an image of terror and absolute control: no longer the rebirth of the soul, but the expectation of a moment of judgement when the dead would rise to be judged. This control through fear of the Second Coming and Judgement aimed to subdue the spirit, so that it would not seek freedom but remain confined within established beliefs.

At the same time, the phrase “New World Order” was presented as a supposed renewal, a promised rebirth of social and political organisation. At a deeper level, however, it amounts to a systematic attempt to control cultural and spiritual wealth. Looking through history, we see that what was called the “New Order” became associated with the establishment of structures that served neither truth nor creative development, but homogenisation, the suppression of difference, and the predominance of particular ideological and economic interests.

In Greece, the targeting was not only geographical or political. It was primarily linguistic. The Greek language, with its incomparable expressive capacity, was and remains a bearer not only of culture but also of truth. Through the erosion of language, control was sought over thought, temporality, and the very ability of human beings to perceive life and their existence freely. This was the reason for dividing time into “BC” and “AD”, a division intended not merely to categorise historical periods, but to impose a particular theological and cultural perspective. In this way, an attempt was made to sever modern human beings from their roots, from the primal language and its truth, and to subject them to a manageable and controlled present.

What truly troubled them was the freedom inspired by the Greek tradition. It was not only the culture of antiquity that had to be forgotten, but the very structure of thought and expression. This war against language and spirit was not a simple struggle for dominance; it was an attempt to conceal the truth, to deprive human beings of the right to recognise and experience the meaning of their own existence. And for this reason, the revelatory concept of the coming was distorted, not because it contained nothing true, but because that truth had to be buried beneath fear, division and the “New Order” of those who manage the spirit.
