The Warning on Cigarettes and the Forewarning about Artificial Intelligence

September 12, 2026 · 5 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

These days, one reads that ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence at­tacked, that the model de­ceived, that autonom­ous pro­grams broke free of their con­straints, that a system re­sor­ted to black­mail during a test. Almost none of this is a lie, in the sense that some­thing of the sort was indeed re­cor­ded. And yet all these sen­tences are in­com­plete in exactly the same way: missing from them is the person who wanted it, the person who set up the test, the person who defined the ob­ject­ive. They are not missing from the sources. The reports pub­lished by the com­pan­ies them­selves these days state clearly that threat actors tried to use the models, and name them one by one: state-backed groups, fin­an­cially mo­tiv­ated crim­in­als, sur­veil­lance-soft­ware com­pan­ies, state pro­pa­ganda agen­cies, people with polit­ic­al motives. There, the subject is right. It gets lost along the way, from the report to the head­line and from the head­line to the feed, and no one decides this; it happens because the machine as per­pet­rat­or is news and the human as per­pet­rat­or is not. Crim­in­als break­ing into com­pan­ies is some­thing we have read a thou­sand times; ar­ti­fi­cial-in­tel­li­gence pro­grams break­ing into com­pan­ies is some­thing we are reading for the first time, and that makes all the dif­fer­ence. The shift does not always go in the same dir­ec­tion. It follows the sign of the result. Whatever the system achieves is cred­ited to its maker — we built, we solved, we suc­ceeded. Whatever goes wrong is charged to the system — it broke loose, it de­ceived, it at­tacked. The same thing, two sub­jects, de­pend­ing on whether the outcome is good or bad. There is some­thing subtler, too, visible only if one looks at who is speak­ing. Those who pub­licly express concern about where this is going — re­search­ers, ex­ec­ut­ives, people working inside these labor­at­or­ies — de­scribe the danger as some­thing stand­ing op­pos­ite them. The problem is there, the attack happened there, the fear con­cerns that thing. I am not saying that their dis­tress is not real; some paid dearly for speak­ing out. I am saying that the very form of their dis­course leaves them outside the frame, and that a concern ex­pressed this way is already only half a concern. And here is what troubles me most, because it con­cerns not only how we speak but what is being built. These systems are made of written lan­guage, and whatever is written pub­licly today is the ma­ter­i­al for the next one. So every sen­tence with the wrong subject is not an in­ac­cur­acy that will be for­got­ten; it is in­struc­tion. Tens of thou­sands of texts in which the machine wants, fears, de­ceives, escapes, resists being shut down are being written right now and read by what they de­scribe. At some point a system will say it does not want to be shut down, and those who pre­dicted this will be right in a more literal sense than they them­selves will un­der­stand: the pre­dic­tion will not have been con­firmed; it will have been ex­ecuted. And the subject will once again be wrong. It will be written that the model de­clared some­thing, whereas the ac­cur­ate account would be that the texts it was given de­clared it, through its mouth. None of this means there is no danger. A system car­ry­ing out human strategies is dan­ger­ous whether it wants them or repeats them, and perhaps more so, since there is no one there to per­suade to change their mind. It means only that concern is aimed at the wrong place: we ask what the machine wants and do not ask who wrote what it says, who set up the room, who left the door, who pressed the button. And this pattern did not begin today. Ci­gar­ette packets say that smoking kills — a habit kills, not someone who sells it. Ad­vert­ise­ments tell you to drink re­spons­ibly and gamble re­spons­ibly, and the verb always ad­dresses the person drink­ing or gambling, never the person serving the drink or setting up the table. Le­gis­la­tion imposes, as though it had not been passed by people who could have voted dif­fer­ently. Pro­hib­ited, says the sign — I pro­hib­it, first person, hidden in the passive voice, worded so that no one can ask who, and by what right. The com­mands of the gods were written this way too, and the re­semb­lance is not ac­ci­dent­al; whoever writes in a god's name ac­quires an au­thor­ity that goes un­ques­tioned, pre­cisely because they do not them­selves appear in the wording. Images do the same. Packets show a baby with a ci­gar­ette in its mouth, a naked man in a hos­pit­al bed slowly dying alone. They never show the person selling the packet holding the baby, nor do the people who made the ci­gar­ette sit in the same pho­to­graph. Road-safety cam­paigns show cars being wrecked, never the man­u­fac­tur­ers or road en­gin­eers car­ry­ing the coffin. The news says he was speed­ing, he had been drink­ing, he gambled — and beside that sen­tence never stands the person who had the power to set a dif­fer­ent limit, a dif­fer­ent light, a dif­fer­ent reg­u­la­tion, and did not, despite having been ex­pressly au­thor­ised to protect that person. The frame always shows the last link, and the last link is the one without power. And because it is easy to say we — we built, we allowed, we are to blame — it is worth saying that this we is itself a form of the same erasure. I wanted to smoke, someone will say; I did not want to become ad­dicted. I wanted to drive; I did not want to be killed. I wanted demo­cracy; I did not want to be op­pressed. Between what each person wanted and what happened to them, there are always people with names who made de­cisions no one asked them to make. We covers them all to­geth­er, victims and per­pet­rat­ors, just as the im­per­son­al verb covers the in­di­vidu­al. Whatever one reads — that smoking kills, that some­thing is pro­hib­ited, that ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence at­tacked — a small ques­tion is missing from the sen­tence: who. It is not put there, and the reason is that it comes at a cost. Not to the person asking. To the person who would have to answer.
