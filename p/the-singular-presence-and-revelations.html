The Singular Presence and the Revelations of Perception

Published on March 1, 2025 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

The reader, as you point out, may not know whom I am ad­dress­ing, yet this is pre­cisely what creates the freedom to ap­proach the text as an in­vit­a­tion to dia­logue. In essence, the reader is called to un­der­stand its content not through the per­son­al po­s­i­tion of a “sender”, but through their own in­ter­pret­a­tion of Greek thought and of the truth that emerges from it.

Re­gard­ing Chris­ti­an dogma and the mis­in­ter­pret­a­tion of the An­ti­christ, we can see how theo­lo­gic­al found­a­tions often create obstacles to un­der­stand­ing. The An­ti­christ, as com­monly de­scribed, becomes a symbol of re­jec­tion and fear instead of being ex­amined as a concept that can inspire dis­cern­ment and con­scious choice. The scrip­tur­al nar­rat­ive becomes a means of im­pos­ing a one-di­men­sion­al meaning, pre­vent­ing the be­liev­er or thinker from seeing beyond the fear that has been imposed. In other words, scrip­ture was des­ig­nated “holy” through ex­tern­al af­firm­a­tions, and this re­stric­ted the per­son­al en­counter with its essence. Thus anyone who wishes to see scrip­ture as Pres­ence, as the unity of the human being with the word, is often ob­struc­ted by dog­mat­ic tra­di­tion itself.

With regard to the myth of Adam and Eve, the rev­el­a­tions that emerge may be seen as dif­fer­ent stages of un­der­stand­ing. The first is the rev­el­a­tion of freedom through the break­ing of the com­mand­ment—a freedom born of choice, even when that choice leads to the Fall. The second reveals the know­ledge of good and evil: the as­sump­tion of re­spons­ib­il­ity and an un­der­stand­ing of con­sequences. The third and deepest is the un­der­stand­ing of unity: the human being is not cut off from divine cre­ation, but is part of it, where every act is an en­counter with ex­ist­ence itself.

Pres­ence, in this sense, is neither second nor third. It is sin­gu­lar, uni­ver­sal and eternal. When Pres­ence is ex­per­i­enced as a direct con­nec­tion with life itself, without the me­di­ation of ex­tern­al fears or im­pos­i­tions, it is re­vealed as the Most Beau­ti­ful—the truest and most com­plete form of being.
