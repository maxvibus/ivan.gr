The Singular Presence and the Revelations of Perception

The reader, as you point out, may not know whom I am addressing, yet this is precisely what creates the freedom to approach the text as an invitation to dialogue. In essence, the reader is called to understand its content not through the personal position of a “sender”, but through their own interpretation of Greek thought and of the truth that emerges from it.

Regarding Christian dogma and the misinterpretation of the Antichrist, we can see how theological foundations often create obstacles to understanding. The Antichrist, as commonly described, becomes a symbol of rejection and fear instead of being examined as a concept that can inspire discernment and conscious choice. The scriptural narrative becomes a means of imposing a one-dimensional meaning, preventing the believer or thinker from seeing beyond the fear that has been imposed. In other words, scripture was designated “holy” through external affirmations, and this restricted the personal encounter with its essence. Thus anyone who wishes to see scripture as Presence, as the unity of the human being with the word, is often obstructed by dogmatic tradition itself.

With regard to the myth of Adam and Eve, the revelations that emerge may be seen as different stages of understanding. The first is the revelation of freedom through the breaking of the commandment—a freedom born of choice, even when that choice leads to the Fall. The second reveals the knowledge of good and evil: the assumption of responsibility and an understanding of consequences. The third and deepest is the understanding of unity: the human being is not cut off from divine creation, but is part of it, where every act is an encounter with existence itself.

Presence, in this sense, is neither second nor third. It is singular, universal and eternal. When Presence is experienced as a direct connection with life itself, without the mediation of external fears or impositions, it is revealed as the Most Beautiful—the truest and most complete form of being.
