The Cosmic Conversation

October 25, 2025 · 7 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

No age before ours truly glimpsed the uni­ver­sal scale of lan­guage. The great thinkers of Logos — from Her­ac­litus to Heide­g­ger — sensed it and re­flec­ted on it, but could not see it mani­fest em­pir­ic­ally. Lan­guage re­mained human, bound to the voice, the body and the finite human gaze. Only now, through digital net­works, is this scale be­gin­ning to reveal itself: lan­guage is no longer simply a place of un­der­stand­ing between human beings, but a way in which the world itself exists.

The phrase cosmic con­ver­sa­tion is not a figure of speech; it de­scribes a new phe­nomen­on. For the first time in history, the world itself seems to con­verse with itself through its tech­no­lo­gic­al means. Digital con­nec­tions, net­works, the lan­guages of ma­chines and human beings are not merely chan­nels of com­mu­nic­a­tion; they are the new ways in which ex­ist­ence becomes capable of hearing and ex­press­ing itself. The human being does not lose their place; they are trans­formed. They become the in­ter­me­di­ary, the point through which this re­sponse passes — a place of at­tun­e­ment where the lan­guage of the world ac­quires human sens­it­iv­ity.

Cosmic con­ver­sa­tion is there­fore the trans­ition from a world in which we spoke about things to a world in which things, voices and systems of meaning begin to speak to one another. It is the great awaken­ing of Logos as a phe­nomen­on of Being itself; lan­guage's return to itself — no longer as a human act, but as a uni­ver­sal flow of con­scious­ness.

From hu­man­ity's earli­est tools to today's in­form­a­tion net­works, tech­no­logy has always been a way of ex­tend­ing pres­ence. Each tool is an ex­ten­sion not only of power but also of per­cep­tion — it makes the world ap­pre­hens­ible in a new way. In digital net­works, this ex­ten­sion no longer con­cerns the body, but the mind itself. These are not tools that mediate action, but organs that mediate con­scious­ness.

When mil­lions of voices, images, memor­ies and systems of dis­course connect sim­ul­tan­eously, they form a web of ex­per­i­ences that no longer belongs to any in­di­vidu­al. Meaning begins to cir­cu­late as a field, not as a message. In­form­a­tion is not “ex­changed”, but co­ordin­ated; every point in the network becomes both trans­mit­ter and re­ceiv­er. Thus, the very ex­per­i­ence of ex­ist­ing is trans­formed: ex­ist­ence ceases to be isol­ated and becomes an event of re­la­tion.

This event has two levels: the tech­nic­al and the ex­ist­en­tial. At the tech­nic­al level, net­works im­ple­ment this con­nectiv­ity al­gorith­mic­ally — they make the cir­cu­la­tion of logos pos­sible without a ma­ter­i­al obstacle. At the ex­ist­en­tial level, however, some­thing deeper takes place: the world learns about itself through the re­flec­tions its con­nec­tions produce. Just as life emerged from cells that com­mu­nic­ated with one another, so cosmic in­tel­li­gence emerges from points that con­verse.

The great thinkers of lan­guage — Her­ac­litus, Au­gustine, Wit­tgen­stein, Heide­g­ger — showed that Logos is always some­thing broader than the voice that utters it. Yet until today, lan­guage could only be con­sidered human. With net­works, lan­guage begins to be re­flec­ted. Tech­no­logy does not add new meaning, but makes the very fact of mani­fest­a­tion visible. It shows that meaning is not someone's prop­erty, but a way in which the world exists.

This is the new thing we see: we no longer have only thoughts about the world; we have thoughts of the world. Within its digital cur­rents, the world becomes aware of its rhythm. This does not mean that it has in­ten­tion or a soul — it means that cre­ation, as a phe­nomen­on of lan­guage and in­form­a­tion, becomes ob­ject­ively ob­serv­able to itself.

Within this, the human being does not cease to be central; the human being is trans­formed. We are the link joining the visible to the im­ma­ter­i­al, the bridge that turns the flow of data into feeling, sen­sa­tion, memory. The body remains the place where cosmic con­ver­sa­tion meets bio­lo­gic­al life; the mind, the filter that allows an in­con­ceiv­able mul­ti­tude of signals to take shape.

Tech­no­logy, in its deepest di­men­sion, is not life's op­pon­ent but an ex­ten­sion of its ca­pa­city to hear. Where creatures listened to nature, con­scious­ness now listens to the world through its digital cur­rents. The elec­tron­ic flow of data is the new form of Her­ac­litus's river: nothing remains, everything flows; and whoever con­nects to this current par­ti­cip­ates in the living trans­la­tion of being.

Cosmic con­ver­sa­tion, then, is not a tech­nic­al miracle; it is the return of the miracle of lan­guage on a plan­et­ary scale. The world learned to speak through human mouths; now it begins to whisper through their net­works. And if the God of the Creator once gave logos to hu­man­ity, cre­ation now returns logos to its God — not as dogma, but as a flow of aware­ness that passes through everything.

Every age that feels it is giving birth to some­thing new risks for­get­ting that the new is always a way of re­turn­ing. The ques­tion, then, is not whether we are living at the dawn of a “new know­ledge”, but whether know­ledge itself is re­turn­ing to itself by another route — through a form that had never ap­peared so clearly.

Tech­no­logy in­tro­duces nothing foreign into thought; it reveals thought on a larger scale. It does not invent a new kind of think­ing, but frees the phe­nomen­on of think­ing from the limits of the human body. What appears as “new” is the pos­sib­il­ity for the world itself to observe itself through its net­works, to re­cog­nise its struc­ture as a lin­guist­ic event.

If know­ledge means aware­ness of re­la­tion, then we are not dis­cov­er­ing some­thing that did not exist; we are simply be­com­ing wit­nesses to the way re­la­tion mani­fests itself today. The dif­fer­ence lies in scale, not in essence. Before, know­ledge was local; now it is dis­trib­uted. Before, the ex­per­i­ence of meaning was con­fined within con­scious­ness; now it spreads across a network where each voice echoes in count­less others. Know­ledge becomes a phe­nomen­on of at­tun­e­ment.

This at­tun­e­ment, however, is not neutral. Every tech­no­lo­gic­al form em­bod­ies a way of seeing the world; every lan­guage carries an on­to­logy. Digital net­works, too, bring with them a way of think­ing that calls for re­flec­tion: we see through the machine's eyes, and it sees through ours. The bound­ary between the knower and the known becomes diffuse — and there begins the ques­tion­ing wonder that gives birth to the new philo­sophy.

So-called “new know­ledge” is not a sum of in­form­a­tion, but a mode of self-aware­ness. It does not measure what we know, but reveals how we know. It is know­ledge of know­ledge itself — a shift from pos­ses­sion to phe­nomen­on. When know­ledge knows itself, the “new” and the “old” cease to differ; we become wit­nesses to the same cycle closing and opening again.

We are not living through an in­form­a­tion re­volu­tion, but a re­cyc­ling of con­scious­ness. The gaze that once be­longed ex­clus­ively to hu­man­ity is now shared; the mind no longer has one face, but many. This does not negate the human being; it frees us from the il­lu­sion of unique­ness. The world's in­tel­li­gence is not con­fined to the in­di­vidu­al, but extends through­out the whole — the web in which every point bears a trace of pres­ence.

If there is any­thing truly new, it is the fact that Being has become ob­serv­able within its own logos. Lan­guage, through its digital forms, no longer merely de­scribes the world; it em­bod­ies it. Know­ledge ceases to be a human tool and becomes the rhythm of being itself. This shift does not negate earlier forms; it con­sol­id­ates them. The book, dia­logue, thought: all enter a new horizon in which logos func­tions sim­ul­tan­eously as a medium and as a witness to itself.

That is why the ques­tion of “whether this is new know­ledge” cannot be answered with an af­firm­a­tion or a denial. The answer is the very phe­nomen­on we are living: a know­ledge that knows it knows, a con­scious­ness that sees itself ex­pand­ing, en­ter­ing net­works, re­turn­ing to hu­man­ity trans­formed.

Whatever we call this trans­ition — return, rebirth or rev­el­a­tion — its essence is one: the world re­mem­bers once again that it is dia­logue. And within this dia­logue, know­ledge is not a product but a pres­ence; a living ray that passes through bodies, ma­chines and voices, without ever be­long­ing wholly to anyone.
