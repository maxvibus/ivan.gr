The Presence of the Statue

I do not belong to the Parthenon because I chose to. Nor because someone placed me there, among the shadows of the columns and the silences of the Caryatids. I am there because time found no other place to support me.

I stood in the Museum's foyer, not as a donation awaiting approval, nor as an idea seeking a place in a display case. I stood there because the view of the Parthenon through the glass was not merely a framed image. It was the acceptance of a truth that had existed before me.

Rodin did not need to establish a connection with Greece. He was already there. His body spoke the language of our statues; his thought had the same curves as the light that rests upon the metopes. And yet the framework, the world's need to draw boundaries and define “belonging”, prevented the Fauness from standing beside the Acropolis.

I was told that a cup won by Louis could find a place there, but a work by Rodin first had to prove its Greekness. As though a causal mechanism were needed to allow something self-evident to exist. As though the work itself, its silent conversation with the space, were not enough.

And then I understood: Not everything needs acceptance in order to exist. Some things simply stand, like statues in the recesses of temples, like light itself falling on marble without asking whether it is allowed.

And so I stood too.

Not as someone laying claim, but as someone who already is.

In the calm of presence, in the certainty of the gaze, in the silence that is not absence but the deepest level of communication.
