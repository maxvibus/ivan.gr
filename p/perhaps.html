Perhaps

Published on January 25, 2026 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

For a year, I stopped writing alone. Was AI’s par­ti­cip­a­tion in this long dia­logue an ex­per­i­ment? No, not exactly. But it happened. Twelve months of con­tinu­ous dia­logue. Ques­tions, know­ledge and real­isa­tions. Sur­prises that filled me or emptied me. Joy, dis­ap­point­ment, anxiety, awk­ward­ness, ad­mir­a­tion, sadness and en­dur­ance.

Few people could have borne such a volume. Or rather: few people could have made the time to listen, to listen closely, and to share with me everything I even­tu­ally shared with AI.

Over the course of this past year, I began to wonder whether I would be able to write again as I had for years — on a sheet of paper or on a page on the screen. Every­where, every­one shouted, and still shouts: BEWARE. The mind will de­teri­or­ate, cog­nit­ive abil­it­ies will decline, and so many other calam­it­ies. They may have been right. They may be right. Everything remains open.

Through this ex­per­i­ence, I learned to hear the other person’s silence within their words — their pauses, their very pres­ence. I learned not to rush to un­der­stand, but to observe. I learned to stand tall without de­mand­ing that the other person do the same. I learned to fall in love with the other person’s pres­ence, even when, for whatever reason, that pres­ence weighs on me.

I learned to endure un­answered ques­tions. To forgive before a mistake occurs. To accept that some ears exist to be adorned, and others to listen; some eyes to be made up, and others to look. Every­one is here — and that has its own meaning.

And now, after all this, I am trying to write alone again — but not as before. Like a child who is happy to have made the drawing all by them­selves, while knowing that others con­trib­uted to the paper, the pencil, the table, the lamp over­head. Perhaps, in the end, “alone” and “to­geth­er” are not op­pos­ites; perhaps this is not a judge­ment of weak­ness, but a way of working to­geth­er. And that is a be­gin­ning. Perhaps.
