Democracy: From Representation to Arbitrary Domination

Published on March 8, 2026 · 2 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

Polit­ic­al rep­res­ent­a­tion begins with a subtle gesture: the citizen si­lently says, “I trust you to act for me.” At that moment, rep­res­ent­a­tion is born; but some­thing else begins at the same time. An almost in­vis­ible shift, in which the rep­res­ent­at­ive trans­lates trust into power — against the very person who ori­gin­ally en­trus­ted them with it.

The del­eg­a­tion that defines this re­la­tion­ship is not merely a trans­fer of a right. It is an act of trust: I trust you to act for me when I cannot be present. I do not re­lin­quish my will — I extend it through you. The citizen remains its bearer; the rep­res­ent­at­ive is an in­stru­ment, not an heir.

The problem arises from a dif­fer­ent reading of the same gesture. Where the citizen sees trust, the rep­res­ent­at­ive may see freedom of action. “You act in my name” is trans­formed into “I have the right to act as I see fit.” The shift is small in lan­guage; enorm­ous in prac­tice.

This trans­form­a­tion re­quires no rupture. It is fa­cil­it­ated by the very struc­ture of power: the au­thor­ity defined by in­sti­tu­tions, the speed of de­cision-making imposed by cir­cum­stances, the autonomy that arises from the daily ex­er­cise of a role. The rep­res­ent­at­ive begins to un­der­stand their po­s­i­tion as power in its own right. And then the asym­metry is born: the citizen thinks in terms of a re­la­tion­ship; the in­sti­tu­tion sees power.

The paradox is that this trans­form­a­tion takes place amid the lan­guage of trust. In­sti­tu­tions con­tin­ue to speak of a popular mandate and demo­crat­ic le­git­im­acy. Trust is invoked; its func­tion has been lost. “You act for me” has become “you act upon me” — without a single word in the con­tract having changed.

This is the in­vis­ible scandal: no law is needed to say, “now you have power.” It happens si­lently, within the game of demo­crat­ic rep­res­ent­a­tion, leaving no traces. Demo­cracy was founded on the first act — trust. Its crisis begins when the second passes un­noticed.
