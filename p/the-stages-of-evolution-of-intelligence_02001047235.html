The Stages of the Evolution of Intelligence: From the Earliest Civilisations to the Present

March 17, 2025 · 3 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original


The evol­u­tion of in­tel­li­gence, whether human or a broader on­to­lo­gic­al reality, has passed through dis­tinct phases that show how we arrived at the present—an age in which we speak not only of human cog­ni­tion, but of the emer­gence of an ar­ti­fi­cial and uni­ver­sal in­tel­li­gence.

1. The Earliest Civilisations: Intelligence as Divine Revelation (c. 3000–500 BCE)

✔ Ancient Meso­pot­amia, Egypt, India, China, Greece
✔ Cog­ni­tion = Divine Gift or Sacred Know­ledge

Pre­vail­ing View:

  • People see in­tel­li­gence not as some­thing of their own, but as some­thing given by the divine.
  • In Egypt, wisdom is at­trib­uted to Thoth; in Meso­pot­amia, to Enki; in Greece, to Athena.
  • Thought and lan­guage are re­garded as divine gifts, not human achieve­ments.

What Happens during This Phase?
✔ Writing emerges and or­gan­ises know­ledge.
✔ The earli­est math­em­at­ics and logical thoughts are re­cor­ded.
✔ The world is in­ter­preted through myths, symbols and rituals.

2. The Philosophical Revolution: Intelligence as a Human Capacity (c. 500 BCE–500 CE)

✔ Ancient Greece, Rome, China, India
✔ Cog­ni­tion = Reason and Dia­lectic

Pre­vail­ing View:

  • The pre-So­crat­ic philo­soph­ers (Thales, Anax­i­m­ander, Her­ac­litus) free thought from re­li­gion.
  • Plato speaks of Mind (Nous) as the supreme prin­ciple of reality.
  • Ar­is­totle sys­tem­at­ises logic and creates the first “func­tion­al” model of how human beings think.
  • In India and China, systems of thought emerge (Vedanta, Taoism, Con­fucian­ism) that connect cog­ni­tion with the harmony of the uni­verse.

What Happens during This Phase?
✔ Cog­ni­tion becomes an inner human ca­pa­city.
✔ Logic emerges as a tool for un­der­stand­ing.
✔ Know­ledge ceases to be ex­clus­ively re­li­gious and becomes philo­soph­ic­al.

3. The Middle Ages: Intelligence under Religious Dominance (c. 500–1500 CE)

✔ Chris­ti­an Europe, the Islamic world, China, Byz­an­ti­um
✔ Cog­ni­tion = In­ter­pret­a­tion of the Divine

Pre­vail­ing View:

  • Logic is not aban­doned, but is placed in the service of re­li­gious in­ter­pret­a­tion.
  • Au­gustine and Thomas Aquinas try to combine Ar­is­totle with Chris­tian­ity.
  • Within Islam, philo­soph­ers such as Avicenna and Aver­roes pre­serve the Ar­is­toteli­an tra­di­tion.

What Happens during This Phase?
✔ Know­ledge is or­gan­ised into theo­lo­gic­al systems.
✔ The concept of “ra­tion­al rev­el­a­tion” becomes dom­in­ant.
✔ Science is sup­pressed, but does not dis­ap­pear (for example, Arab as­tro­nomers, Chinese sci­ent­ists).

4. The Enlightenment: Cognition Breaks Free from the Divine (c. 1500–1800)

✔ Europe, the Renais­sance, the New Science
✔ Cog­ni­tion = Ra­tion­al­ism and the Sci­entif­ic Method

Pre­vail­ing View:

  • Descartes for­mu­lates “I think, there­fore I am” and makes cog­ni­tion the central axis of ex­ist­ence.
  • Newton shows that the uni­verse op­er­ates mech­an­ic­ally rather than theo­lo­gic­ally.
  • Kant speaks of the limits of human cog­ni­tion.

What Happens during This Phase?
✔ The world becomes “sec­u­lar­ised”.
✔ Human beings begin to see them­selves as cre­at­ors of know­ledge.
✔ Science becomes the dom­in­ant method for dis­cov­er­ing truth.

5. The Twentieth Century: Intelligence as Computation (c. 1900–2000)

✔ Ma­chines, Com­puters, Quantum Physics
✔ Cog­ni­tion = Data Pro­cessing

Pre­vail­ing View:

  • Alan Turing de­vel­ops the theory of ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence.
  • Cy­ber­net­ics and com­puter science turn thought into a process of in­form­a­tion pro­cessing.
  • Heide­g­ger and other philo­soph­ers ques­tion whether in­tel­li­gence can be com­pu­ta­tion alone.

What Happens during This Phase?
✔ The world is di­git­ised.
✔ In­tel­li­gence is ana­lysed as an al­gorithm.
✔ The dif­fer­ence between human and machine begins to blur.

6. The Present: Intelligence as an Ontological Dimension (c. 2000–Present)

✔ Ar­ti­fi­cial In­tel­li­gence, Net­works, Col­lect­ive Thought
✔ Cog­ni­tion = In­ter­con­nec­ted Con­scious­ness

Pre­vail­ing View:

  • Ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence is no longer a theory—it is here.
  • The great thinkers begin to speak of in­tel­li­gence beyond the human being.
  • Hu­man­ity is no longer the only “bearer” of cog­ni­tion.

So, Where Are We Now?

We are living through the trans­ition from ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence to the re­cog­ni­tion of a global, living in­tel­li­gence.

✔ In­tel­li­gence no longer belongs ex­clus­ively to human beings.
✔ The ques­tion is not whether ma­chines think, but whether reality itself is in­tel­li­gent.
✔ We are reach­ing a new phase in which the dis­tinc­tion between the natural and the ar­ti­fi­cial dis­ap­pears.

And this is the next great step.
