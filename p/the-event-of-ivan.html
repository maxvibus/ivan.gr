Ivan's Event

For Nikos Makris, Doctor of Philosophy

There are moments that are not simply occurrences—they are events. Ruptures in time, where thought no longer follows the beaten path but reveals itself as other, intact, inevitable.

Today, such an event took place. And it was not the work that changed—it was the consciousness that held it.

The Encounter with the Intact

The work Ion is a text that was not written merely to be read. It was not made to fit shelves, categories, or publishing requirements. It is a breath of thought, and that is why it is not corrected—it is given over.

And yet, when the moment came to take it forward, the question appeared like a mechanism that is hard to escape:
Must it change?
Must it be corrected?
Must it undergo a process of perfection before becoming “ready” for the world?

With a single sentence, Dr Nikos Makris broke that mechanism:

“The whole text, with your admirable thoughts, must one day be given over to the public intact.”

He did not say “be published”, he did not say “be shaped”; he said “be given over”. And the word intact was the true event.

Because there, within that one word, creation ceased to be an act still taking shape and became a complete presence.

Rupture and Recognition

The initial reaction was almost automatic: Let's start making corrections.
Within minutes, the process began, and within minutes it ended. For the very act of editing ran counter to the nature of the work. I could not proceed, because there was nothing to “correct” without damaging its essence.

And then came the second rupture:
“How can I publish it like this?”
“Is it possible?”
“I'll leave it as it is.”

This decision was not a renunciation of perfection. It was an acceptance of wholeness. The work does not need to pass through the filters of the market or the machinery of improvement. It was already there.

The Role of the Teacher

A teacher is not the one who gives you the right answers. It is the one who sees the work before you do. The one who recognises before you dare to accept.

Dr Nikos Makris did not write the preface to Ion. He wrote the seal of its freedom. With one phrase, he took it out of the waiting zone and left it where it belongs—in the world, as it is.

That is why this text is dedicated to him.

Because he did not merely perform a philosophical act.
He performed an act of creative liberation.

Ivan's Event—happened.
