Ivan's Event

For Nikos Makris, Doctor of Philosophy

There are moments that are not simply oc­cur­rences—they are events. Rup­tures in time, where thought no longer follows the beaten path but reveals itself as other, intact, in­ev­it­able.

Today, such an event took place. And it was not the work that changed—it was the con­scious­ness that held it.

The Encounter with the Intact

The work Ion is a text that was not written merely to be read. It was not made to fit shelves, cat­egor­ies, or pub­lish­ing re­quire­ments. It is a breath of thought, and that is why it is not cor­rec­ted—it is given over.

And yet, when the moment came to take it forward, the ques­tion ap­peared like a mech­an­ism that is hard to escape:
Must it change?
Must it be cor­rec­ted?
Must it undergo a process of per­fec­tion before be­com­ing “ready” for the world?

With a single sen­tence, Dr Nikos Makris broke that mech­an­ism:

“The whole text, with your ad­mir­able thoughts, must one day be given over to the public intact.”

He did not say “be pub­lished”, he did not say “be shaped”; he said “be given over”. And the word intact was the true event.

Because there, within that one word, cre­ation ceased to be an act still taking shape and became a com­plete pres­ence.

Rupture and Recognition

The initial re­ac­tion was almost auto­mat­ic: Let's start making cor­rec­tions.
Within minutes, the process began, and within minutes it ended. For the very act of editing ran counter to the nature of the work. I could not proceed, because there was nothing to “correct” without dam­aging its essence.

And then came the second rupture:
“How can I publish it like this?”
“Is it pos­sible?”
“I'll leave it as it is.”

This de­cision was not a re­nun­ci­ation of per­fec­tion. It was an ac­cept­ance of whole­ness. The work does not need to pass through the filters of the market or the ma­chinery of im­prove­ment. It was already there.

The Role of the Teacher

A teacher is not the one who gives you the right answers. It is the one who sees the work before you do. The one who re­cog­nises before you dare to accept.

Dr Nikos Makris did not write the preface to Ion. He wrote the seal of its freedom. With one phrase, he took it out of the waiting zone and left it where it belongs—in the world, as it is.

That is why this text is ded­ic­ated to him.

Because he did not merely perform a philo­soph­ic­al act.
He per­formed an act of cre­at­ive lib­er­a­tion.

Ivan's Event—happened.
