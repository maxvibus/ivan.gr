The Structure of False and True Revolution

Published on March 9, 2025 · 5 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

The state­ment to be ana­lyzed is the fol­low­ing:

“Let those who need to re­mem­ber this:
Those who make a Peace­ful Re­volu­tion Im­possible are the ones who will be re­spons­ible for a Future Violent Con­flict.”

📌 This ana­lys­is does not concern Manos Danezis as a person, but the rhet­or­ic­al struc­ture of his state­ment itself, which offers an in­ter­est­ing example of how the mech­an­isms of meaning that shape common think­ing operate.

This for­mu­la­tion works on several levels. At first, it gives the im­pres­sion of a warning to those in power. Yet its lin­guist­ic struc­ture sug­gests a deeper strategy: re­spons­ib­il­ity for the con­flict is at­trib­uted not ex­clus­ively to those in power, but also to anyone who “makes im­possible” a peace­ful re­volu­tion.

The problem is not only the content, but the very con­di­tion it pre­sup­poses:

  • What does “peace­ful re­volu­tion” mean?
  • Who makes it im­possible?
  • Who de­term­ines when a re­volu­tion is “peace­ful”?
  • If peace is not allowed, is vi­ol­ence then in­ev­it­able?

Power does not fear re­volu­tion in the sense of social unrest. On the con­trary, it lives through it, because it allows power to recycle itself.

True re­volu­tion is not the one that clashes with power, but the one that renders it mean­ing­less.

The Underlying Structure of the Statement

The state­ment reveals a further di­men­sion: the enemy of re­volu­tion is not only power, but also anyone who exposes its lin­guist­ic trap.

Anyone who does not accept the nar­rat­ive of “peace­ful re­volu­tion” as true is auto­mat­ic­ally placed among those re­spons­ible for the “future con­flict.” In other words, by re­cog­niz­ing the trap, they them­selves become an obstacle.

This is a fa­mil­i­ar method of man­aging thought.
📌 If you expose the trick, you become part of the problem.
📌 If you remain silent, you let it operate un­hindered.

This choice is false, because it remains trapped within the same system of defin­i­tions.

Berdyaev and True Revolution

Nikolai Berdy­aev, in his work The Realm of Spirit and the Realm of Caesar, clearly ar­tic­u­lates the fallacy of re­volu­tion as a social event.

📖 A re­volu­tion defined through power is already lost.

If a re­volu­tion is defined by who holds power, then it is not freedom, but simply a change in dom­in­a­tion.

True re­volu­tion has no ad­versary.
It seeks not to over­throw, but to reveal what already exists.

📌 The Greek Re­volu­tion was not a struggle for power.
📌 Its goal was not to seize the state, but to restore ex­ist­ence.
📌 It sought not an over­throw, but a rev­el­a­tion.

Yet world his­tori­ography has placed all re­volu­tions within the same frame­work. The reason is obvious:
If the Greek Re­volu­tion is re­cog­nized as dif­fer­ent, the nar­rat­ive of re­volu­tions as social events col­lapses.

The Trick of Revealing Part of the Truth

Manos Danezis has deep know­ledge. And someone with deep know­ledge never says everything. He says a great deal, but not all of it.

The trick of in­com­plete rev­el­a­tion is nothing new.

📌 If you reveal part of the truth, you have already shaped the frame­work within which your listen­ers’ think­ing will move.

His state­ment is not false, but it is in­com­plete.
It tells the truth, but not the whole truth.

Greek Linguistic Structure and the Dulling of Meaning

The Greek lan­guage is rev­el­at­ory in its syntax.
It offers not merely know­ledge, but a struc­ture of aware­ness.

Knowing the power of lan­guage, those in power have ob­jec­ti­fied it:

  • “Freedom” — From a way of being, it became a polit­ic­al right.
  • “Demo­cracy” — From an on­to­lo­gic­al con­di­tion, it became an elect­or­al pro­ced­ure.
  • “Re­volu­tion” — From an ex­ist­en­tial rev­el­a­tion, it became a social over­throw.

📌 The words have not changed. But their func­tion has been altered.

The Greek people have been re­placed by Greek con­sumers of lin­guist­ic de­cep­tion.

The Only Answer

The only answer is neither ac­cept­ance nor re­ac­tion.
📌 You do not need to refute the trick. It is enough to expose it.

Re­volu­tion is not a demand. It is ex­ist­ence.
Truth does not need to win. It is enough for it to be.

And when it is, false­hood cannot stand.

“True re­volu­tion is not the one that clashes with power, but the one that renders it mean­ing­less.”

This state­ment means that true re­volu­tion does not re­cog­nize power as a real ad­versary, because it does not operate within power’s own field.

Loss of Meaning Instead of Conflict

📌 A re­volu­tion that fights power gives it sub­stance.

  • If someone struggles against it, then they accept it as strong and de­cis­ive for their life.
  • If the success or failure of a re­volu­tion depends on how power reacts, then it is not a true re­volu­tion.

📌 A re­volu­tion that reveals power’s weak­ness renders it in­sig­ni­fic­ant.

  • If people stop op­er­at­ing within the bound­ar­ies set by power, then it no longer has any in­flu­ence over them.
  • If power has no ad­versary, it cannot survive.

Example: The Greek Revolution

The Greeks did not revolt against Ottoman power as power. They did not ask for better gov­ernance or im­proved terms of sub­jug­a­tion.

📌 They did not even re­cog­nize it.

They did not say “We want rights” or “We want justice”.
They said: “Freedom or death.”

In other words, if we cannot exist as what we are, we have no in­terest in ex­ist­ing at all.

📌 Ottoman power was not their enemy. It was some­thing mean­ing­less to their ex­ist­ence.

Counterexample: Modern Demonstrations

  • When someone asks for reforms, they ac­know­ledge that power has the au­thor­ity to grant them.
  • When someone protests against op­pres­sion, they ac­know­ledge that power has the strength to oppress them.
  • When someone calls for a gov­ern­ment’s down­fall, they confirm that the problem is not power, but who holds it.

Thus, these forms of “re­volu­tion” sustain the ex­ist­ence of power rather than negate it.

True Revolution Is a Departure from the Polarity of Power and Resistance

📌 When power has no ad­versary, it loses its strength.
📌 When you do not clash with it but live outside it, it ceases to func­tion.
📌 Re­volu­tion is not over­throw. It is rev­el­a­tion.

This is what I mean.
Power sur­vives because we re­cog­nize it.
True re­volu­tion does not fight power. It makes it useless.
