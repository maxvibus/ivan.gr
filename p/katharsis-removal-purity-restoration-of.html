Katharsis, Removal, Purity — The Restoration of Meanings

March 5, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

Let us pause over the words, where the Greek language bears its own truth.

Katharsis is not removal.
Purity is not purging.
And clarity is not merely transparency, but a path towards understanding.

Katharsis: The Path towards What Is Unalloyed

The word Katharsis (with the final -s, hastily removed in common usage) does not mean simply removing impurities.
It is not a mechanical process of cleansing; it is not the disposal of unwanted elements.

Katharsis is recognition, acceptance, the dissolution of what is superfluous, and the emergence of what is essential.

  • It is revelation, not erasure.
  • It is elevation, not removal from one’s place.
  • It is an inner light, not external coercion.

Aristotle knew this well when he spoke of the katharsis of the soul through art.
He was not speaking of purging, violence, or expulsion.
He was speaking of an experience that leads to inner peace.

Purging: The Transformation of Katharsis into Violence

Purging is a term that resembles katharsis, but it is not the same.
Purging became associated with forcible removal—political, social, or financial.

Where there is purging, there is expulsion, rejection, punishment.
There is no deliverance, no disclosure, but the annihilation of what has been judged superfluous.

Purging leaves you no room to stand and look.
It pushes you violently out of the picture instead of allowing you to see it differently.

And so we have come to say that we will “clean someone out” when we mean that we will destroy them.

Purity: The Presence of What Is True

Purity is not simply the absence of stains.
It is not merely hygiene or order.
It is the presence of the essence, without distractions, without superfluous elements that distort what is true.

Purity does not remove—it reveals.
It does not erect barriers; it opens passages.
It is not defence; it is freedom.

And What Became of Clarity?

They gave the name Diavgeia, “Clarity,” to a bureaucratic website.
As if clarity meant merely making something publicly visible.

Yet clarity is not transparency—it is understanding.

  • Clear is the gaze that has not been clouded.
  • Clear is the meaning that is not imposed through terms, but recognised without effort.
  • Clear is the feeling that is not burdened by pointless anxiety.

If clarity does not lead to the katharsis of consciousness, it is not clarity—it is merely exposure to light without an understanding of light.

Eirōn, Heron, or Simply Truth?

Today, to be ironic means to mock, to question deceitfully.
But in antiquity the eirōn was not someone who mocked others.

He was the one who pretended not to know in order to bring the truth to light.
Socrates was the greatest eirōn, because through feigned ignorance he brought forth true knowledge.

And what of the name Heron?

Heron of Alexandria, who saw mechanics as an expression of wisdom.
Heron, who showed that science can be wonder, not control.

What, then, are eirōn and Heron?

Perhaps a challenge to return to the purity of meanings.

Katharsis, Not Salvation

Katharsis is not salvation from something.
It is not an external act that comes to save.
It is the recognition of truth without fear, without the need for atonement.

Katharsis is not punishment.
It is not deliverance from sin; it is the restoration of existence to its true measure.

Katharsis makes no demands.
It simply happens.

Not through imposition,
but with the calm of the rising light.
