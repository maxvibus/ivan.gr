Iōn: The Fissure and the Current

February 28, 2025 · 1 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

It is not a story. It is not an ar­gu­ment. It is the moment when some­thing shifts.

The work of Iōn is not a nar­rat­ive with a be­gin­ning, middle and end. It is a phe­nomen­on, a dif­fu­sion of voice, body, faith and doubt. Speech here belongs to no one· not to Iōn himself, nor to Apollo, nor to So­crates as he ques­tions. It belongs to the current that passes through them all.

There is a fissure at the centre of the work. Where Iōn does not know whether it is he who speaks or someone else within him. Where know­ledge is not his own, yet is not foreign either. His speech is not a tech­nique, yet neither is it ac­ci­dent­al. It is some­thing in between.

And this “in-between” is the world that opens here.

The first text does not explain. It does not close mean­ings. It simply opens the space for the flow.
