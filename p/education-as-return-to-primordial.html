Education as a Return to the Primordial Principle

March 1, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

Education, as we often understand it, is not simply a process of accumulating degrees or credentials. It is, rather, a return, a reconnection with that primordial principle which makes us human. In Greek thought, “paideia” does not mean knowledge in the narrow sense alone, but cultivation—the cultivation of the soul, character, and an understanding of the essence of our existence.

When we say that degrees do not make the person, we mean that formal certificates are not enough to touch the essence of being human. Knowledge of the Greek principle, of that first impulse towards the search for truth, reminds a person who they truly are. This knowledge does not “turn” them into something else; on the contrary, it offers them the means to dissolve the layers of ignorance, fear and prejudice that may have covered them.

This reminder is indeed forceful, because it disrupts the inertia of habit and compels a person to look beyond the surface. At the same time, it is gentle, because it imposes nothing foreign, but brings the person back to their own self. It is like rediscovering a forgotten voice within, a voice that was always there but can now be heard more clearly and understood more deeply.

Thus, the discerning reader who encounters this approach will not feel that they must “become something else”. Instead, they will feel that they are remembering something they already are. And it is this remembrance that brings their humanity back to life. It is the Greek principle whispering that knowledge is not the storage of information, but a path towards a deeper understanding of oneself and one’s relationship with the world.
