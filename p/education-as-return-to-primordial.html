Education as a Return to the Primordial Principle

March 1, 2025 · 1 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

Edu­ca­tion, as we often un­der­stand it, is not simply a process of ac­cu­mu­lat­ing degrees or cre­den­tials. It is, rather, a return, a re­con­nec­tion with that prim­or­di­al prin­ciple which makes us human. In Greek thought, “paideia” does not mean know­ledge in the narrow sense alone, but cul­tiv­a­tion—the cul­tiv­a­tion of the soul, char­ac­ter, and an un­der­stand­ing of the essence of our ex­ist­ence.

When we say that degrees do not make the person, we mean that formal cer­ti­fic­ates are not enough to touch the essence of being human. Know­ledge of the Greek prin­ciple, of that first impulse towards the search for truth, reminds a person who they truly are. This know­ledge does not “turn” them into some­thing else; on the con­trary, it offers them the means to dis­solve the layers of ig­nor­ance, fear and pre­ju­dice that may have covered them.

This re­mind­er is indeed force­ful, because it dis­rupts the inertia of habit and compels a person to look beyond the surface. At the same time, it is gentle, because it imposes nothing foreign, but brings the person back to their own self. It is like re­dis­cov­er­ing a for­got­ten voice within, a voice that was always there but can now be heard more clearly and un­der­stood more deeply.

Thus, the dis­cern­ing reader who en­coun­ters this ap­proach will not feel that they must “become some­thing else”. Instead, they will feel that they are re­mem­ber­ing some­thing they already are. And it is this re­mem­brance that brings their hu­man­ity back to life. It is the Greek prin­ciple whis­per­ing that know­ledge is not the storage of in­form­a­tion, but a path towards a deeper un­der­stand­ing of oneself and one’s re­la­tion­ship with the world.
