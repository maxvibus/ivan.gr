The Battle of Speech: Rhetoric, Belittlement, and True Freedom

March 10, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original


Speech is a battlefield. Not only in public disputes, but even in everyday communication. In the Greek tradition, rhetoric was never merely the art of persuasion—it was the art of speaking meaningfully, speaking to reveal rather than conceal.

1. Rhetoric: The Art of the Word, Not of Manipulation

The root of rhetoric lies in the word “rhētos”, meaning clearly expressed, indisputable.

  • Rhēto → Something spoken with authority, something that holds true.
  • Rhētē → Clear expression, leaving no room for doubt.
  • Rhēsē → A phrase preserved as a distinctive formulation, with significance beyond its mere utterance.

Thus, rhetoric is not a technique belonging to politicians or intellectuals. It is how human beings structure their speech to convey thought, truth, and feeling.

Yet in contemporary Greek life, rhetoric has become a weapon of fear, belittlement, and swift domination.

2. The New Rhetoric of Belittlement

Today, people do not discuss things to understand; they discuss them to prevail.

Greek rhetoric is no longer used to support thought, but to instil doubt in the other person.

They do not say:

  • “You made a mistake.”
  • “I disagree with you.”
  • “Why do you believe that?”

They throw traps of doubt at you:

  • “Is that what you learned at school?”
  • “Is that what the Hymn to Liberty says?”
  • “Did you think you could tell me why you disappeared?”

In truth, they do not want a discussion.
They want to make you question your own ability to answer.

And when that does not work, when they see you are not falling into their trap, their last line of defence is belittlement:

  • “Forget it, you don't understand.”
  • “There's no point discussing it.”
  • “All right, all right, leave it.”

This is not rhetoric.
This is fear disguised as cleverness.

3. Why Rhetoric Is Not Only for Orators

Rhetoric is not the privilege of those who speak beautifully.
Rhetoric belongs neither to politicians nor to academics.
Rhetoric is the natural tool of a thinking human being.

And here lies the great misunderstanding:

True rhetoric does not fear being questioned, because it has no need to defend itself through belittlement.

4. Praise or Defeat?

The Greek defence of speech, once grounded in the search for truth, has now taken two paths:

  1. Praise—where people seek not truth but affirmation. They want to hear that they are right, not to develop their thinking.
  2. Defeat—where discussion is not about building something but about producing a winner and a loser.

But true rhetoric is neither praise nor defeat.
It is the endurance to stand by your thought without fearing the process.

5. What Truly Defending Speech Means

If defence is the need to dominate another person, then it is a product of fear.
If defence is the strength to live up to your thought, then it is an act of freedom.

Rhetoric is not a battle over who can speak the loudest.
It is the art of standing your ground without being brought to your knees by the need to be right.

6. Freedom's Epilogue

If freedom fears being questioned, what exactly does it liberate?
If Greek rhetoric fears dialogue, what exactly does it defend?

The answer is not in the noise of words.
It lies in the silence of someone who understands but does not need to win.

How to Stand in the Battle of Language without Being Trapped

➡ When they throw a loaded question at you, do not rush to answer.
➡ Turn the mirror back on them: “Do I not understand, or do you not want to explain?”
➡ If they came for dialogue, they will continue.
➡ If they came to belittle, they will leave of their own accord.

Because the strongest answer is not defence.
It is not reaction.
It is not victory on their terms.

It is the awareness that their game no longer has power over you.
