The Unsettling Truth and the Defense That Is Not a Retreat

Published on March 5, 2025 · 3 min read

AI-as­sisted trans­la­tion from the Greek. Greek original

I know that what I say may be unsettling.

But I do not say it in order to un­settle.
And yet, even I am un­settled by the fact that it un­settles others.

It is not pro­voca­tion.
It is not op­pos­i­tion for op­pos­i­tion's sake.
It is the dis­tinc­tion between dis­com­fort that seeks revenge and dis­com­fort that seeks un­der­stand­ing.

What is dis­com­fort?

  • It is a sign that some­thing touches deeply.
  • It is the real­iz­a­tion that some­thing cannot remain un­ex­amined.
  • It is the unease before the in­ev­it­ab­il­ity of un­der­stand­ing.

It is not a bel­li­ger­ent stance.
It is not an ac­cus­a­tion.
It is the point where two paths meet and are called upon to speak.

Accusation and Distance

Degrees are cat­egor­ies.
Not only in courts, but also in thought.

Judg­ment, dis­tinc­tion, sep­ar­a­tion.
Where does natural dis­tinc­tion end and hos­til­ity begin?

The Greek mindset does not see the “between” as a violent dis­tance.
The between is the point of con­nec­tion.

  • It is not “me and the ad­versary.”
  • It is “me and the other person, who have not yet un­der­stood one another.”
  • It is not a space of hos­til­ity.
  • It is a space of pos­sible friend­ship.

Friend­ship in Greek thought is not born of the absence of con­flict,
but of the choice to un­der­stand.

Not as a weapon.
Not as an ob­lig­a­tion.
But as what the heart of every creature needs:

Un­der­stand­ing, respect, love, unity of heart.

Giving an Account and the Need to Explain

What does “apo­logoumai”—to give an account of oneself—mean?

It means I speak about what con­cerns me.
It does not mean I submit to someone else's judg­ment.
It does not mean I apo­lo­gize out of fear.

It means I put into words how I see some­thing.
Not to be vin­dic­ated, but so that my truth may be spoken.

What if I was wrong?
What if I caused hurt?

I do not give an account in order to di­min­ish myself.
I give an account because ex­plan­a­tion matters more than the verdict.

Speak­ing out is not revenge.
It is not the need for someone to be pun­ished.
It is the need for the event to be ac­know­ledged before it becomes a wound that will not heal.

Revenge and Adjudication

What does “ek­dikasi”—ad­ju­dic­a­tion—mean?

Ek + Dikē.
Whatever must be brought before Dikē, justice.
Not to be pun­ished.
Not to be an­ni­hil­ated.
But to be il­lu­min­ated, ac­know­ledged, given its true place.

Dikē is not revenge.
Dikē is not hatred.
Dikē is the res­tor­a­tion of harmony.

Not the harmony of silence that hides the problem.
But the harmony of truth spoken, with no further need for war.

Mechanics as Divine Work

The one who spoke of mech­an­ics as divine work was Hero of Al­ex­an­dria.
He saw the per­fec­tion of the laws that move the uni­verse.

Mech­an­ics is not merely tools.
It is wisdom that reveals the harmony of nature.

The same holds for con­cepts.

  • When you un­der­stand a concept cor­rectly, it ceases to be a burden.
  • When you discern the truth behind a word, it ceases to be a weapon.

Ex­plan­a­tion matters more than con­sol­a­tion.
Un­der­stand­ing matters more than an apology.

Because truth needs no defense.
It needs to be spoken clearly, without fear.
