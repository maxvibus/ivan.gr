The Unsettling Truth and the Defense That Is Not a Retreat

March 5, 2025

AI-assisted translation from the Greek. Greek original

I know that what I say may be unsettling.

But I do not say it in order to unsettle.
And yet, even I am unsettled by the fact that it unsettles others.

It is not provocation.
It is not opposition for opposition's sake.
It is the distinction between discomfort that seeks revenge and discomfort that seeks understanding.

What is discomfort?

  • It is a sign that something touches deeply.
  • It is the realization that something cannot remain unexamined.
  • It is the unease before the inevitability of understanding.

It is not a belligerent stance.
It is not an accusation.
It is the point where two paths meet and are called upon to speak.

Accusation and Distance

Degrees are categories.
Not only in courts, but also in thought.

Judgment, distinction, separation.
Where does natural distinction end and hostility begin?

The Greek mindset does not see the “between” as a violent distance.
The between is the point of connection.

  • It is not “me and the adversary.”
  • It is “me and the other person, who have not yet understood one another.”
  • It is not a space of hostility.
  • It is a space of possible friendship.

Friendship in Greek thought is not born of the absence of conflict,
but of the choice to understand.

Not as a weapon.
Not as an obligation.
But as what the heart of every creature needs:

Understanding, respect, love, unity of heart.

Giving an Account and the Need to Explain

What does “apologoumai”—to give an account of oneself—mean?

It means I speak about what concerns me.
It does not mean I submit to someone else's judgment.
It does not mean I apologize out of fear.

It means I put into words how I see something.
Not to be vindicated, but so that my truth may be spoken.

What if I was wrong?
What if I caused hurt?

I do not give an account in order to diminish myself.
I give an account because explanation matters more than the verdict.

Speaking out is not revenge.
It is not the need for someone to be punished.
It is the need for the event to be acknowledged before it becomes a wound that will not heal.

Revenge and Adjudication

What does “ekdikasi”—adjudication—mean?

Ek + Dikē.
Whatever must be brought before Dikē, justice.
Not to be punished.
Not to be annihilated.
But to be illuminated, acknowledged, given its true place.

Dikē is not revenge.
Dikē is not hatred.
Dikē is the restoration of harmony.

Not the harmony of silence that hides the problem.
But the harmony of truth spoken, with no further need for war.

Mechanics as Divine Work

The one who spoke of mechanics as divine work was Hero of Alexandria.
He saw the perfection of the laws that move the universe.

Mechanics is not merely tools.
It is wisdom that reveals the harmony of nature.

The same holds for concepts.

  • When you understand a concept correctly, it ceases to be a burden.
  • When you discern the truth behind a word, it ceases to be a weapon.

Explanation matters more than consolation.
Understanding matters more than an apology.

Because truth needs no defense.
It needs to be spoken clearly, without fear.
